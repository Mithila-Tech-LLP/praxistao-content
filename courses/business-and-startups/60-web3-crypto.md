# Chapter 60: Web3 and Crypto — What's Real and What's Hype

*Crypto and Web3 attracted billions in investment and produced genuine innovation in some areas, and extraordinary fraud in others. This chapter cuts through both the hype and the cynicism to explain what's actually happening.*

---

## What Is Blockchain and Why Does It Matter?

Before discussing crypto, you need to understand the underlying technology.

**The core problem blockchain solves:** How do two parties who don't trust each other transact without a trusted intermediary?

**Traditional solution:** Trust a bank. If Rahul wants to pay Priya ₹1,000, Rahul's bank debits ₹1,000, Priya's bank credits ₹1,000. The banks are the trusted intermediaries that verify the transaction and maintain the ledger.

**The blockchain solution:** What if the ledger was maintained by thousands of computers simultaneously? No single entity controls it. Every transaction is recorded on every participating computer. To change the ledger, you'd need to simultaneously change it on a majority of all computers worldwide — computationally impossible.

This is a "distributed ledger" — a database with no central owner, maintained by consensus.

**Why this is theoretically important:**
- Transactions without intermediaries (no bank fees, no 3-5 day clearing times)
- Programmable money (smart contracts — code that automatically executes transactions when conditions are met)
- Asset ownership without a central registry (land records, stock ownership, identity)

## Bitcoin (2009) — The Original

Satoshi Nakamoto (an unknown person or group) published a white paper in 2008 titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System."

**Bitcoin's purpose:** Digital cash that doesn't require banks. If you want to send value across the world to someone you've never met, you can do it in minutes with Bitcoin, with no bank, no government, no intermediary.

**Who actually uses Bitcoin:**
- Store of value ("digital gold") — the primary use case. People hold Bitcoin as an inflation hedge.
- International remittances — sending money across borders cheaply
- Sanctions evasion (Russia, Iran, North Korea — this is real and controversial)
- Criminal transactions (a much smaller share than media suggests; most criminals prefer cash)

**The supply cap:** Only 21 million Bitcoin will ever exist. This is programmed into the protocol. Compare this to dollars, rupees, and euros, which central banks can print unlimited amounts of.

Bitcoin believers argue: if global adoption grows and supply is fixed, Bitcoin price must rise. This is the "digital gold" thesis.

**Bitcoin's biggest problems:**
- Energy consumption (Bitcoin mining uses as much electricity as a small country)
- Slow transaction speed (7 transactions per second vs Visa's 24,000 TPS)
- No recovery if you lose your private key — millions in Bitcoin has been lost forever

## Ethereum (2015) — Programmable Blockchain

Vitalik Buterin was 19 when he wrote the Ethereum white paper in 2013 (published 2015).

**The innovation:** Ethereum added "smart contracts" to blockchain. A smart contract is a program that runs on the blockchain — automatically executing when predefined conditions are met.

**Example smart contract:** "If Player A deposits 1 ETH and Player B deposits 1 ETH, whoever wins this chess match automatically receives both ETH."

No bank, no escrow company, no trust required — the code enforces the contract.

**What smart contracts enable:**
- DeFi (Decentralized Finance) — financial services without banks
- NFTs (Non-Fungible Tokens) — unique digital ownership records
- DAOs (Decentralized Autonomous Organizations) — companies governed by code and token holders

---

## DeFi — Finance Without Banks

Decentralized Finance uses Ethereum smart contracts to replicate financial services:

**Lending/Borrowing (Aave, Compound):**
- Deposit crypto → earn interest
- Borrow against your crypto holdings
- No bank account needed, no credit check — just collateral

**Decentralized Exchanges (Uniswap, Curve):**
- Trade crypto directly with other users, no centralized exchange
- Automated market makers (AMMs) use algorithms instead of order books

**Yield farming/staking:**
- Provide liquidity to protocols and earn fees
- Complex strategies to maximize yield

**The appeal:** In countries with terrible banks, capital controls, or currency hyperinflation (Venezuela, Argentina, Nigeria), DeFi offers an alternative.

**The reality:**
- Most DeFi activity is speculation, not genuine financial inclusion
- Smart contract bugs have caused billions in losses (hacks, exploits)
- Regulatory clarity is absent in most countries
- The actual "unbanked" population in developing countries rarely uses DeFi — it's too technically complex

## NFTs — The Peak Hype Moment (2020-2022)

**What an NFT is:** Non-Fungible Token. A blockchain record proving you own a specific digital file.

If you buy an NFT of a digital artwork, the blockchain records: "This wallet address owns this specific JPEG." The NFT doesn't give you copyright, it doesn't give you the ability to delete copies — it just records your ownership on the blockchain.

**The NFT boom:**
- 2021: NFT trading volume reached $25 billion
- Bored Ape Yacht Club (profile picture NFTs) sold for $150,000+ each
- Jack Dorsey sold his first tweet as an NFT for $2.9 million
- NBA Top Shot: officially licensed NBA video clips as NFTs (generated $700M+ in sales)
- Celebrities (Paris Hilton, Justin Bieber, Eminem) bought and promoted NFTs

**The collapse:**
- By 2023, NFT trading volume was down 97%+ from peak
- Most NFTs are essentially worthless
- The average NFT sold for $150 in 2021; by 2023, the average sale price had collapsed

**What was real:** The concept of provable digital ownership has genuine use cases — event tickets, game items, music rights. The speculative bubble obscured the legitimate applications.

## FTX — The Biggest Fraud in Crypto History

**Sam Bankman-Fried (SBF)** built FTX into the second-largest crypto exchange in the world. He was celebrated as a visionary, on the cover of Forbes and Fortune, and was the second-largest donor to the Democratic party.

FTX collapsed in November 2022 when it emerged that:
- FTX customer funds ($8 billion) had been secretly lent to Alameda Research (SBF's trading firm)
- When Alameda lost money in the crypto downturn, it couldn't repay the funds
- When customers tried to withdraw from FTX, the money wasn't there

SBF was arrested, tried, and convicted of fraud. Sentenced to 25 years in prison.

**The aftermath:**
- Millions of ordinary people lost money
- Institutional investors (Sequoia, SoftBank, Tiger Global) wrote off their investments
- The "effective altruism" movement that SBF had publicly championed suffered reputational damage

**The lesson:** In unregulated markets, fraud is much easier. The crypto industry's resistance to regulation directly enabled the FTX fraud.

## What's Real in Crypto

Separating signal from noise:

**Actually valuable:**
- Bitcoin as digital gold / inflation hedge — genuine demand from institutional investors
- Stablecoins (USDT, USDC) — dollar-pegged crypto, used heavily for cross-border remittances
- Blockchain for supply chain — Walmart uses blockchain to track food safety
- Digital identity and credentials — tamper-proof records of qualifications
- Smart contracts for financial contracts between sophisticated parties

**Potentially valuable:**
- Cross-border payments (reduce 3-7% bank fees to 0.1-1%)
- Tokenization of real-world assets (real estate, art, private equity)
- Gaming economies with real-money item trading

**Mostly hype:**
- Most altcoins — 95%+ of coins have no genuine use case
- "Web3" social networks replacing Facebook — not happening
- NFTs as investment vehicles
- The "metaverse" running on blockchain

## India and Crypto

**The Indian regulatory journey:**
- 2018: RBI banned banks from crypto transactions — most Indian exchanges shut down
- 2020: Supreme Court reversed the RBI ban
- 2022: India imposed 30% flat tax on crypto gains + 1% TDS on transactions
- The 30% tax + 1% TDS significantly reduced Indian crypto trading volume

**Indian exchanges:**
- CoinDCX
- CoinSwitch Kuber
- WazirX (now complicated after parent Binance issues)
- Mudrex

**The global context:** India's crypto taxation is among the harshest in the world. Most developed countries tax crypto as capital gains (15-20%); India taxes at 30% with no offset for losses.

**UPI vs crypto for payments:** For Indian domestic payments, UPI does everything crypto promises — instant, free, widely accessible. Crypto has no advantage for domestic Indian payments. The use case in India is primarily speculation and cross-border.

## Key Lessons

**1. Technology can be genuinely innovative and mostly used for speculation.** Blockchain is real technology. 90% of its use has been speculation.

**2. "Decentralization" often isn't.** Most crypto projects have founders, foundations, and insiders who hold most of the tokens. True decentralization is hard.

**3. Regulatory clarity matters.** The FTX fraud happened partly because the industry resisted regulation. Better regulation would have caught SBF earlier.

**4. The underlying problem matters.** Crypto solves the "trusted intermediary" problem. In countries with trustworthy banks and stable currencies (India, US), this is less urgent. In Venezuela or Argentina with hyperinflation, it matters more.

**5. Separate the technology from the tokens.** Blockchain as a database technology has genuine enterprise use cases. Cryptocurrency tokens are a separate (and mostly speculative) asset class.

---

## Summary

- Blockchain is a distributed ledger that enables transactions without trusted intermediaries — genuinely useful technology
- Bitcoin is digital gold: fixed supply, inflation hedge, cross-border value transfer
- Ethereum added smart contracts: programmable finance, NFTs, DAOs
- The NFT boom of 2021 was largely speculative; collapsed 97%+ by 2023
- FTX was an $8 billion fraud; SBF convicted and sentenced to 25 years
- India's 30% flat tax makes crypto unattractive for Indian investors; UPI is superior for domestic payments
