# Chapter 50: Apple — From Near-Bankruptcy to the Most Valuable Company on Earth

*In 1997, Apple had 90 days of cash left. In 2024, it crossed $3 trillion in market value. No turnaround story in business history compares.*

---

## Origin Story — The Garage (1976)

Steve Jobs, 21, and Steve Wozniak, 25, founded Apple Computer Company in Jobs's parents' garage in Los Altos, California. A third co-founder, Ronald Wayne, sold his 10% stake back for $800 — one of the most expensive decisions in business history.

Wozniak was the engineer — a genuine genius who designed the Apple I and Apple II from scratch. Jobs was the visionary and salesman — who saw the commercial potential of Wozniak's technology before anyone else did.

**The Apple II (1977)** was the first mass-market personal computer. Before it, computers were kits for hobbyists. The Apple II came pre-assembled, with a keyboard, color display, and built-in software. It was a computer you could plug in and use.

It made Apple the dominant personal computer company in the late 1970s.

---

## The First Rise and the Fall (1980-1985)

**The Apple III (1980):** Apple's first major failure. Overheating, reliability issues. Job lot required pulling all 14,000 units from the market.

**The Lisa (1983):** First commercial computer with a graphical user interface (GUI) and mouse. Revolutionary technology, priced at $9,995 (~$30,000 in today's money). A commercial failure, but an important prototype for what came next.

**The Macintosh (1984):** Introduced in the most famous commercial in history — the 1984 Super Bowl ad. The Mac brought the GUI to a broader audience at a lower price. Iconic design, revolutionary interface.

But the Mac had problems: expensive, slow, no software. And Steve Jobs was becoming impossible to work with.

**The Board Fires Steve Jobs (1985):** After a power struggle with CEO John Sculley (recruited from Pepsi by Jobs himself), the Apple board sided with Sculley and removed Jobs from his role. Jobs resigned and left the company he had founded.

Apple without Jobs: produced a string of mediocre products (PowerBook, Newton). By the mid-1990s, Apple was losing hundreds of millions per year and had 90 days of cash left.

---

## The Wilderness Years — NeXT and Pixar (1985-1997)

Jobs didn't disappear. He founded two companies:

**NeXT:** A workstation computer for education and business. Beautiful hardware, innovative software (the NeXT operating system would become the foundation of macOS). Commercial failure as hardware. But the software and the engineering talent Jobs assembled were invaluable.

**Pixar:** Jobs bought Pixar from Lucasfilm for $5 million in 1986 when it was a struggling hardware company. Under Jobs's stewardship (and the creative genius of John Lasseter), Pixar made Toy Story (1995) — the first fully computer-animated feature film. Pixar became the most successful animation studio in history. When Disney acquired Pixar in 2006, Jobs became Disney's largest individual shareholder.

**What Jobs learned in the wilderness:**
- How to build a culture of excellence (NeXT's engineering culture)
- How storytelling and emotion are central to products (Pixar)
- Patience and resilience (12 years out of Apple)
- The importance of software and ecosystem (NeXT OS became macOS)

---

## The Return and the Turnaround (1997-2001)

**The acquisition:** In 1997, desperate Apple acquired NeXT for $427 million to get a modern operating system. Jobs came back as "iCEO" (the "i" standing for "interim").

**The first 90 days:**
- Met with Bill Gates and secured a $150 million investment from Microsoft (keeping Apple alive and settling a patent dispute)
- Cut Apple's product line from 70 products to 4 (a 2×2 matrix: consumer/professional × desktop/portable)
- Fired most of the executive team
- Began the "Think Different" campaign — repositioning Apple as the brand for creatives and rebels

The quote that captured the moment: "The products suck! There's no sex in them anymore!"

**Think Different:** The most important marketing decision Apple made. The campaign didn't feature any products — it featured creative icons (Einstein, Gandhi, Picasso, Martin Luther King Jr.) with the tagline "Think Different." It repositioned Apple from a failing computer company to a symbol of creativity and nonconformity.

**The iMac (1998):** Jobs's first product back. Designed by Jony Ive. Translucent candy-colored plastic. A single cable. Beautiful and fun. It said: computers don't have to be beige boxes. The iMac sold 278,000 units in its first six weeks. Apple was back.

---

## The iPod Revolution (2001)

In 2001, music piracy was destroying the music industry. Napster had 80 million users downloading music for free.

Jobs saw an opportunity: legal digital music, beautifully designed, seamlessly integrated with software.

**The iPod:** "1,000 songs in your pocket." Beautiful scroll wheel. 5GB storage. Simple design. Launched October 2001.

**iTunes:** The store that made it work. 99 cents per song. No subscription. Easy to buy legal music. Jobs negotiated with all five major music labels to allow digital sales — a feat that took months of negotiation and his personal credibility.

The iPod + iTunes created Apple's first ecosystem: device + software + content. This model would define everything Apple built afterward.

By 2004, iPod had 70% of the digital music player market. Apple was no longer just a computer company.

---

## The iPhone (2007) — The Most Important Product Launch in History

On January 9, 2007, Steve Jobs walked onto a stage and said: "Every once in a while, a revolutionary product comes along that changes everything. And Apple has been very fortunate — it's been able to introduce a few of these into the world."

He then showed three things:
1. A widescreen iPod with touch controls
2. A revolutionary mobile phone
3. A breakthrough internet communicator

"These are not three separate devices. This is one device. And we are calling it iPhone."

The audience erupted.

**What the iPhone replaced:**
- Dedicated music players (iPod)
- Feature phones (Nokia's business)
- PDAs (Palm Pilot's business)
- Point-and-shoot cameras (eventually)
- GPS devices (eventually)
- Paper maps

**The competitors' reaction:**

Nokia's CEO at the time called it "not a very significant" product. BlackBerry's co-CEO said the iPhone would fail because it had no physical keyboard. Steve Ballmer at Microsoft laughed and said: "$500 fully subsidized with a plan? I said that is the most expensive phone in the world and it doesn't appeal to business customers."

Within 4 years:
- Nokia went from 50% global phone market share to almost nothing
- BlackBerry went from 20% smartphone market share to essentially zero
- Palm went bankrupt
- Microsoft's Windows Mobile was destroyed

**The App Store (2008):** When Apple opened the App Store, they created an entirely new economy. Developers could build and sell apps on iPhone, with Apple taking 30%.

By 2024: $1 trillion+ in app sales since launch. 4 million+ apps. App Store generates $30-35 billion per year for Apple.

---

## Apple After Steve Jobs — Tim Cook's Operational Excellence

Steve Jobs died in October 2011 from pancreatic cancer. He was 56.

Many predicted Apple would decline without him. Instead, Apple under Tim Cook became the most valuable company in the world.

**What Cook did differently:**

**Operational mastery:** Jobs was product. Cook is supply chain. Cook optimized Apple's supply chain to be the most efficient in the world — single-source relationships with key suppliers, massive purchase commitments that created exclusivity, tight integration between hardware and manufacturing.

**Services expansion:** Apple TV+, Apple Music, Apple Pay, Apple Arcade, iCloud, App Store. Combined, "Services" generates $100B+ per year and growing faster than hardware.

**China discipline:** Cook negotiated extraordinary relationships with Foxconn and other Chinese manufacturers. Apple's China supply chain is its most significant strategic dependency — and risk.

**New product categories:** Apple Watch (launched 2015), AirPods (2016), Apple Silicon (M1, M2, M3 chips replacing Intel — a massive technological bet that succeeded brilliantly).

## Apple's Business Model

**Revenue breakdown (2023):**

| Segment | Revenue | % of Total |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| iPhone | $200B | 52% |
| Services | $85B | 22% |
| Mac | $29B | 8% |
| iPad | $28B | 7% |
| Wearables/Home/Accessories | $40B | 10% |

**The Services flywheel:**

Every iPhone sold creates a potential services customer. 1.5 billion active Apple devices = 1.5 billion potential subscribers to Apple Music, iCloud, Apple TV+, Apple Arcade, Apple Pay.

Services gross margin: ~70-75%. Hardware gross margin: ~35-40%. As services grows, Apple's overall margin expands.

This is the genius of Apple's platform: hardware creates the installed base; services monetize it.

---

## Key Lessons from Apple

**1. Focus is a competitive advantage.** Jobs cut 70 products to 4. Apple today makes fewer products than almost any company at its scale. Every product gets enormous attention.

**2. Vertical integration beats horizontal.** Apple controls the chip (M-series), operating system (iOS/macOS), app store, and often the application itself. This integration creates an experience no horizontal player can match.

**3. Control the customer experience.** Apple Stores replaced every third-party retailer for Apple's primary sales. Apple controls the moment of purchase, the retail environment, the support experience. No gap in the chain.

**4. Design is a competitive moat.** Jony Ive's design team created aesthetic standards that competitors spent decades trying to copy. Being genuinely beautiful is a durable advantage.

**5. The best comeback story.** 90 days of cash left in 1997 to $3 trillion market cap. The lesson: companies that maintain culture, talent, and product excellence can recover from almost anything.

---

## Summary

- Apple was founded in 1976, rose quickly, fell dramatically by 1997, then executed history's greatest turnaround
- The iMac (1998), iPod+iTunes (2001), and iPhone (2007) were the three products that rebuilt the company
- The App Store created an entirely new developer economy with Apple at the center
- Under Tim Cook, Apple became the world's most valuable company through operational excellence and services expansion
- Key lessons: focus, vertical integration, customer experience control, design as moat
