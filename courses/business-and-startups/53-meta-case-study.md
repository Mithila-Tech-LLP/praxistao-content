# Chapter 53: Facebook/Meta — The Social Network and Its Billion-Dollar Consequences

*Facebook connected the world and then discovered that connecting the world has complicated consequences. The story of Meta is the story of social media itself — its extraordinary power and its deep problems.*

---

## The Origin — Harvard and the Saverin Drama

February 2004. Mark Zuckerberg, 19 years old, second-year Harvard student, launched TheFacebook.com for Harvard students only.

Within 24 hours: 1,200 students registered. Within a month: half of Harvard.

**The Eduardo Saverin story:** Saverin was Zuckerberg's co-founder and original CFO, contributing $19,000 in seed funding. When Facebook moved to Silicon Valley and raised from Peter Thiel, Saverin's stake was diluted in a way courts later deemed inappropriate. He received $300 million in the eventual settlement and a Facebook co-founder credit — but the dispute became the central drama of *The Social Network* film.

**Peter Thiel's $500,000:** PayPal co-founder Peter Thiel invested $500,000 for 10% of Facebook in 2004. One of the best angel investments ever — that $500,000 became $1 billion+ at IPO.

## The Slow Build — Facebook vs MySpace

In 2006, MySpace was the world's most visited website. They had 100 million users; Facebook had 12 million. MySpace had signed a $900 million search advertising deal with Google.

**Why Facebook won and MySpace lost:**

**Product design:** Facebook was clean, simple, and required real identity. MySpace was chaotic, allowed HTML customization, and was pseudonymous. Facebook's real-name policy created authentic connections.

**Exclusive strategy:** Facebook started with colleges (only @edu email addresses could register) before opening to everyone. This created quality networks — you actually knew the people on your network.

**News Feed (2006):** Facebook's News Feed — showing a stream of friends' activity — was initially controversial. Users hated it. Protests were organized. But Zuckerberg's insight was correct: people wanted to see their friends' updates, they just didn't know it yet. The News Feed became the central social product of the next decade.

**MySpace's mistake:** News Corp acquired MySpace in 2005 for $580 million and immediately tried to monetize aggressively. The user experience degraded. Users started leaving for Facebook.

By 2008, Facebook surpassed MySpace.

---

## The Mobile Transition — Facebook's Near-Death and Comeback

In 2012, as Facebook prepared for its IPO, a serious problem emerged: mobile.

Facebook's product was built for desktop. The mobile experience was terrible. And users were switching to mobile. If they couldn't transition, Facebook would be left behind.

Zuckerberg made an aggressive move: he declared Facebook a "mobile-first" company and reorganized engineering around it.

**The HTML5 experiment failed.** Facebook tried building a mobile app using web technologies (HTML5). It was slow and unreliable. Engineers called it the "crappiest app."

**The native apps:** Zuckerberg scrapped the HTML5 approach and rebuilt the iOS and Android apps natively. The rebuilt apps were dramatically faster.

Within a year of the IPO, mobile was Facebook's primary platform. Today, 98% of Facebook's daily active users access it on mobile.

## Acquisitions That Defined the Company

### Instagram ($1 billion, 2012)

Instagram had 30 million users, 13 employees, and zero revenue when Facebook bought it for $1 billion in April 2012.

Zuckerberg's reasoning (revealed in internal emails discovered during antitrust proceedings): Instagram was growing faster than Facebook among young users. It was a potential threat. Better to own it than compete with it.

At the time of acquisition, $1 billion seemed outrageous for an app with no revenue and 13 employees.

Today: Instagram has 2 billion+ users and generates $30-40 billion in annual revenue. One of the best acquisitions in history.

**What happened after:** Kevin Systrom and Mike Krieger (Instagram founders) stayed for 6 years, growing Instagram. In September 2018, they resigned, citing tensions with Zuckerberg over product direction and independence.

### WhatsApp ($19 billion, 2014)

WhatsApp had 450 million users, 55 employees, and minimal revenue when Facebook acquired it for $19 billion.

The thesis: messaging was becoming the primary form of communication, particularly in emerging markets (India, Brazil, Indonesia). WhatsApp dominated these markets. If you don't own messaging, you lose the next generation of social.

WhatsApp founders Jan Koum and Brian Acton had explicitly anti-advertising values — WhatsApp's tagline was "No ads! No games! No gimmicks!" Acton later left Facebook and donated $50 million to the Signal Foundation (a private messaging alternative), tweeting "It is time. #deletefacebook." Quite an ending.

Today: WhatsApp has 2+ billion users. India is its largest market (500+ million users). Revenue model is still evolving — primarily through WhatsApp Business APIs.

### Oculus ($2 billion, 2014)

The VR bet. A foundation of the later Metaverse strategy. Still not generating significant revenue a decade later.

## Facebook's Advertising Business — How It Actually Works

Facebook's advertising model is fundamentally different from Google's.

**Google:** Captures explicit intent (you searched for "laptop under ₹50,000" — you want a laptop)

**Facebook:** Infers intent from behavior (you've been looking at laptop reviews, you're 25, in tech, your income suggests you can afford ₹50,000 — you might buy a laptop)

Facebook knows:
- Every post you've liked
- Every page you've followed
- Where you live, where you work
- Who your friends are and what they're interested in
- Your political views (inferred)
- Your relationship status
- Your purchasing behavior (through advertiser pixel data)

This behavioral profile is enormously valuable for advertisers. A brand can say: "Show my ad only to men aged 25-35, in Bangalore, who have recently been interested in fitness products and have disposable income." This targeting precision is impossible in any other medium.

**The revenue:**
Facebook's ad revenue: $117B in 2023.
Revenue per user in the US/Canada: ~$60/year.
Revenue per user in India: ~$3/year.

India has the largest user base (400M+ users) but generates only 1% of Facebook's global revenue — because advertiser spending in India is much lower than the US.

## The Cambridge Analytica Scandal

In 2018, it emerged that Cambridge Analytica (a political consulting firm connected to the Trump campaign) had harvested data from 87 million Facebook users without their explicit consent.

The mechanism: Facebook had an API that allowed third-party apps to collect not just the user's data, but their friends' data. An academic created a personality quiz app; 270,000 people took it; Cambridge Analytica collected data on 87 million people (the users plus their friends).

**The consequences:**
- Zuckerberg testified before Congress and the EU Parliament
- Facebook paid a $5 billion FTC fine (the largest privacy fine in history)
- #DeleteFacebook trended globally
- Growing awareness that social media platforms were using personal data in ways users hadn't understood

**The deeper issue:** Facebook's business model *requires* collecting and using personal data. There is no way to run a targeted advertising business without it. Privacy reform fundamentally conflicts with Facebook's revenue model.

## The Pivot to Meta — The Metaverse Bet

In October 2021, Zuckerberg renamed the company "Meta" and announced the metaverse as the company's primary focus.

**The stated vision:** The metaverse is the next computing platform — a 3D virtual world where people work, socialize, and play using AR/VR headsets. Just as mobile replaced desktop, the metaverse would replace the phone.

**The investment:** Meta has spent $40-50+ billion building Reality Labs (the metaverse division) with minimal revenue.

**What happened:**
- The Quest headset had technical success but limited mainstream adoption
- Avatar Workrooms (virtual offices) were mostly ridiculed
- Horizon Worlds (their social VR world) had sparse, unappealing graphics
- The metaverse vision largely failed to materialize by 2023

**The pivot back:**
In 2023, Zuckerberg pivoted back to "Year of Efficiency" — cutting 21,000 employees (25% of workforce) and refocusing on core social products and AI.

Was the metaverse bet wrong? The technology might matter eventually. The timing appears to have been off by 10-15 years.

## TikTok and the Reels Response

ByteDance's TikTok posed an existential threat to Facebook/Instagram by 2020-2021. Short-form video was dramatically more engaging than anything Meta had.

The response: Instagram Reels. A direct clone of TikTok's format.

**The controversial decision:** Instagram's core users (photographers, lifestyle creators) objected to Instagram becoming a video-first platform. Kylie Jenner tweeted "what happened to instagram?"

The decision was right. Short-form video is where engagement is going. Reels now accounts for 50%+ of time spent on Instagram and a growing share of Instagram's revenue.

## Key Lessons from Meta

**1. Acquire threats before they become existential.** Instagram and WhatsApp could have destroyed Facebook. By acquiring them, Zuckerberg eliminated the threats and gained massive businesses.

**2. Social networks have powerful network effects — and they can't be ported.** Your friends are on WhatsApp; switching to Signal means leaving your friends behind. This is a powerful moat.

**3. The attention economy has real costs.** Facebook's engagement-maximizing algorithms surface emotionally provocative content. The consequences — political polarization, teen mental health, misinformation — are real and well-documented.

**4. Platform control is a double-edged sword.** Facebook owns the distribution for billions of users. This gives extraordinary power but also attracts regulatory scrutiny and public distrust.

**5. Don't bet the company on one vision.** The metaverse bet consumed $50B and significant management attention. A more diversified innovation portfolio might have been better.

---

## Summary

- Facebook won the social network wars through better product (News Feed), real identity, and better mobile transition
- Instagram ($1B) and WhatsApp ($19B) were defensive acquisitions that became the most valuable media businesses in history
- Cambridge Analytica revealed the dark side of targeted advertising: personal data used in ways users didn't understand
- The Meta pivot was an expensive bet on virtual reality that appears premature
- TikTok forced Instagram Reels — a successful clone that may be Meta's most important product today
