# Chapter 48: Deep Tech Startups — AI, Biotech, Climate, and Hard Technology

Deep tech startups are built on scientific breakthroughs or major engineering innovations — not just clever software design. They take longer, cost more, and carry more technical risk. But when they succeed, the outcomes can be transformative.

---

## What Is Deep Tech?

Deep tech is technology that requires a genuine scientific or engineering breakthrough to work — not just better software, a new business model, or a new distribution channel.

**Deep tech examples:**
- New drug molecules (years of discovery, clinical trials)
- Advanced materials (graphene, new battery chemistries)
- Fusion energy
- Robotics (physical manipulation in unstructured environments)
- Quantum computing
- New semiconductor architectures
- Gene editing (CRISPR)

**Not deep tech:**
- A new app for ordering food
- A marketplace connecting freelancers with clients
- A new way to do HR software
- A social network

The distinction matters because deep tech requires different funding, different timelines, and different team compositions.

---

## Why Deep Tech Is Different

**Longer development timelines:** A drug might take 10-15 years from discovery to market. A new battery chemistry might take 5-8 years to productionize. Software apps take months.

**More capital:** You need labs, equipment, researchers with PhDs, clinical trials, regulatory approval. A deep tech startup might spend ₹100 crore before generating any revenue.

**Higher technical risk:** The core technology might not work. Unlike software (where if you build it, it generally works), deep tech bets on scientific hypotheses that might be wrong.

**Longer path to revenue:** Deep tech companies often must demonstrate technology → then build products → then get regulatory approval → then find customers. Each stage takes years.

**The opportunity:** Deeper moats, longer protection from competition (patents, regulatory approvals), and transformative outcomes that change entire industries.

## AI Deep Tech — Beyond Chatbots

Most "AI companies" are application companies — they use existing AI models (OpenAI, Anthropic) to build products. Genuine AI deep tech companies are building new model architectures, new training methods, or new AI hardware.

**Foundation model companies:**
- **OpenAI:** Building GPT-series models. The most-funded AI company ever (~$80B+ raised).
- **Anthropic:** Building Claude. Founded by ex-OpenAI researchers. Safety-focused. $7.3B+ raised.
- **Google DeepMind:** AlphaFold (protein folding), Gemini (LLM), AlphaCode (coding).
- **Mistral (France):** Open-source LLMs. European AI champion.

**Indian AI:**
- **Sarvam AI:** Building AI models for Indian languages. Founded by Vivek Raghavan (former chief of Aadhaar technology). The most important Indian AI company.
- **Krutrim (Ola):** Bhavish Aggarwal's AI push. India's first AI unicorn.
- **AI4Bharat (IIT Madras):** Research organization building NLP tools for Indian languages.

**The Indian language opportunity:** India has 22 official languages and 1,600+ dialects. English AI models serve 100-150 million Indians who are English-comfortable. The next billion users need AI in Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Marathi, Bengali. Sarvam's Indic language models could unlock this market.

## Biotechnology and Healthcare Tech

**Drug discovery:**

Traditional drug discovery: scientists hypothesize a drug molecule → synthesis → lab testing → animal testing → Phase I/II/III clinical trials → FDA/CDSCO approval. Cost: $1-2 billion per drug. Time: 12-15 years.

AI-accelerated drug discovery: AI models screen millions of potential molecules computationally, predicting which will bind to target proteins. This reduces the initial search from years to months.

**AlphaFold:** DeepMind's protein structure prediction model (2021) solved a 50-year biology challenge — predicting the 3D structure of proteins from amino acid sequences. This accelerated drug discovery, materials science, and basic biology research. One of the most important scientific contributions of the decade.

**Gene Editing:**
CRISPR-Cas9 (Nobel Prize 2020) allows scientists to edit DNA with unprecedented precision. Applications: treating genetic diseases (sickle cell disease, muscular dystrophy), creating disease-resistant crops, potentially preventing inherited conditions.

The first CRISPR therapy was approved by the FDA in 2023 (for sickle cell disease). A treatment, not a cure — but a proof that gene editing works.

**Indian biotech:**
- **Biocon:** Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw's company — one of India's largest biotech firms. Biosimilars, novel biologics.
- **Serum Institute of India:** World's largest vaccine manufacturer by volume. Critical during COVID.
- **Dr. Reddy's, Sun Pharma:** Large pharma companies with growing R&D pipelines.

## Space Tech

**SpaceX's disruption:**
Before SpaceX, a rocket launch cost $150-250 million. SpaceX brought this to $60-80 million with Falcon 9, and is targeting $10M with Starship. The key innovation: reusable rockets.

Reusability broke the economics of space. Suddenly, satellites, internet coverage, and space exploration became economically viable at much larger scale.

**Indian space startups:**
- **Skyroot Aerospace:** First Indian private rocket launch (Vikram-S, 2022). Building orbital launch vehicles.
- **Agnikul Cosmos:** Launched the world's first single-piece 3D-printed rocket engine (2024).
- **Dhruva Space, Pixxel, Satellize:** Satellites, Earth observation.

**ISRO and the new Indian space policy:**
India reformed its space policy in 2020-2023, allowing private companies to use ISRO infrastructure and compete with government launches. This triggered the current startup wave.

India's cost advantage in space mirrors its software advantage — Indian engineers can build satellites and launch vehicles for a fraction of US costs, with world-class talent.

## Climate Tech

*(Also covered in Chapter 63 in detail)*

**Why climate tech is deep tech:**
Climate solutions often require genuine scientific breakthroughs — better batteries, cheaper solar cells, carbon capture chemistry, nuclear fusion. These are not software problems.

**The categories:**
- **Clean energy:** Solar, wind, nuclear (SMRs — Small Modular Reactors)
- **Energy storage:** Grid-scale batteries (sodium-ion, flow batteries), hydrogen
- **Carbon capture:** Direct air capture (Climeworks), geological storage
- **Sustainable materials:** Alternatives to steel, cement, plastics
- **Alternative proteins:** Lab-grown meat, insect protein, precision fermentation

**India's climate tech:**
- **Ola Electric:** Bhavish Aggarwal's EV pivot — India's largest EV company by volume. IPO in 2024.
- **Ather Energy:** Tarun Mehta's premium EV scooter company.
- **Greenko, ReNew Power:** Renewable energy companies.
- **Log9 Materials:** Building batteries for Indian EVs with Indian chemistry.

## How to Fund Deep Tech

Deep tech requires different funding than software:

**Government grants:** DST, DBT, BIRAC (biotech), ISRO, DRDO all have programs. Essential for early-stage deep tech — no dilution.

**Non-dilutive funding:** SBIR programs (if targeting US markets), European Research Council, NIF (National Innovation Foundation in India).

**Patient VC:** Some VC firms specialize in deep tech (Khosla Ventures, Breakthrough Energy Ventures, SOSV). They accept longer timelines in exchange for potentially transformative outcomes.

**Strategic investors:** Large corporations (Toyota investing in battery startups, Reliance investing in energy storage, pharma companies investing in biotech) who have strategic reasons to fund development.

**PIPE (Private Investment in Public Equity):** For later-stage deep tech companies that have IPO'd but need additional capital for commercialization.

---

## India's Deep Tech Opportunity

India has specific advantages in deep tech:

- **Cost advantage:** Research is dramatically cheaper in India
- **Science talent:** IISc, IITs, NITs produce world-class researchers
- **Manufacturing ambition:** PLI schemes incentivize manufacturing of complex products
- **Large market:** 1.4 billion people = large test market for new technologies (EVs, vaccines, genomics)

The gap: **commercialization**. India is very good at research and engineering. The challenge is translating research into commercial products, navigating regulatory pathways, and scaling manufacturing. This is improving but still a challenge.

---

## Summary

- Deep tech = genuine scientific/engineering breakthroughs; not just clever software
- Longer timelines (5-15 years), more capital (₹100 crore+ before revenue), higher technical risk
- AI deep tech: foundation model companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Sarvam AI for Indian languages)
- Biotech: AI-accelerated drug discovery, CRISPR gene editing, AlphaFold's impact
- Space tech: SpaceX made space affordable; Indian startups (Skyroot, Agnikul) are following
- Climate tech: batteries, solar, carbon capture — the defining investment theme of the next 30 years
- India has talent and cost advantages; commercialization is the gap to fill
