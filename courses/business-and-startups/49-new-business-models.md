# Chapter 49: New Business Models — Fintech, Edtech, Healthtech, and AgriTech

Every decade produces new business models enabled by new technology. This chapter covers the major new categories that have defined Indian startups in the 2010s and 2020s.

---

## Fintech — Financial Technology Disrupting Finance

Fintech is the most successful startup category in India, enabled uniquely by India's digital infrastructure: Aadhaar (identity), UPI (payments), and India Stack (shared data layer).

### Payments

India's UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is the world's most advanced payments infrastructure. Launched by NPCI in 2016, it enables instant bank-to-bank transfers for free.

UPI processed 14 billion+ transactions per month by 2024. For comparison, the US's Venmo does ~100 million per month.

**Who built on top of UPI:**

**PhonePe:** BHIM-UPI app owned by Flipkart (later spun off). Market leader in UPI with ~48% share. $12B valuation.

**Google Pay:** Google's UPI app. Strong in South India.

**Paytm:** Pre-UPI, Paytm built a wallet-based system. Post-UPI, it faced competition from bank-backed players. Evolved into a broader financial services platform, IPO'd in 2021, and has had a tumultuous journey since.

**Razorpay:** Payment gateway for businesses. The "Stripe of India." Razorpay processes $140B+ annually. India's most valuable fintech company (Series F at $7.5B valuation).

**PhonePe vs Google Pay:**
An interesting story of two large players competing. Google had the brand and global scale; PhonePe had the home-court advantage and Flipkart's merchant network. Both succeeded by growing the overall market.

### Lending

**KreditBee, MoneyTap, EarlySalary:** Personal loans for salaried professionals, disbursed within minutes via app. Use alternative data (salary slip, rent, mobile usage) to underwrite risk.

**Lendingkart, NeoGrowth:** SME lending. Traditional banks underserve small businesses. Fintech lenders use accounting data (Zoho Books, Tally) and bank statements to make faster decisions.

**Bajaj Finance:** Not a startup, but the most successful NBFC story in India. From ₹1 crore in profits in 1990 to ₹14,000 crore by 2023. Bajaj Finance's app-based lending has made it India's most valuable NBFC.

### WealthTech

**Zerodha:** The discount brokerage revolution. Made stock trading accessible and affordable. 6+ million active clients.

**Groww:** Mutual fund investment app, now expanded to stocks, US stocks, F&O. Built with simplicity-first design. ~10 million+ investors. $3B valuation.

**Smallcase:** Theme-based stock investment baskets. Created a new way to invest in Indian equities.

### InsurTech

**Acko:** Founded by Varun Dua. Digital-first general insurance. Car insurance, health insurance, embedded insurance (through Amazon). $1.1B valuation.

**Digit Insurance:** Kamesh Goyal's company — simple, transparent insurance products. India's most successful InsurTech IPO.

## Edtech — The Promise and the Collapse

No sector in Indian startups has had a more dramatic rise and fall than edtech.

### What Worked

**BYJU'S (peak):** Byju Raveendran built India's most valuable edtech company, valued at $22B. Starting with after-school learning apps for K-12, BYJU'S expanded to test prep (Aakash acquisition for $1B), upskilling (Great Learning), and US edtech (Epic).

The app genuinely worked for engaged students. The content quality was high. The problem: the business model depended on aggressive sales, high-pressure tactics, and expensive annual subscriptions.

**Unacademy:** Platform for live online classes with prominent teachers. Created a "celebrity educator" culture. Raised $440M+. Grew fast in COVID.

### What Failed

**The BYJU'S collapse (2022-2024):** A combination of: governance failures (auditors resigned, financial reporting delayed), aggressive acquisitions that destroyed value, unsustainable offline expansion, and regulatory investigations. Went from $22B to NCLT bankruptcy proceedings.

The diagnosis: BYJU'S was a high-pressure sales company masquerading as an edtech company. Revenue was driven by misleading sales tactics, multi-year contracts sold to low-income families who couldn't afford them.

**The edtech myth:** COVID created a temporary surge in edtech adoption as schools closed. Founders and investors extrapolated this as permanent behavior change. It wasn't. When schools reopened, most students preferred in-person learning.

### What Survives

**K-12 supplemental learning:** Apps like Khan Academy, Doubtnut, and Photomath that help with specific doubts

**Professional upskilling:** Coursera, Udemy, LinkedIn Learning — adults paying for career skills. This market is real and growing.

**Test prep:** CAT, UPSC, GATE coaching. The demand is real; the question is how much moves online.

## HealthTech — The Trillion-Dollar Opportunity

India's healthcare system is chronically underserved: doctor-patient ratio far below WHO recommendations, specialists concentrated in metros, insurance penetration under 40%.

Technology has opportunities across the stack:

### Telemedicine

**Apollo 247, mFine, Practo:** Video consultations with doctors. COVID normalized this.

The reality: most Indian patients still prefer in-person consultations for serious issues. Telemedicine works for minor issues and follow-ups.

### Digital Pharmacy

**PharmEasy, 1mg (now Tata 1mg), Netmeds (Reliance):** Online medication ordering and home delivery. Large market (India's pharma retail is ₹1.8 lakh crore).

**1mg's path:** Started as medicine price comparison, evolved to full-stack health platform (teleconsultation, lab tests, medicines). Acquired by Tata for ~$445M in 2021.

### Diagnostics

**Healthifyme:** Fitness and nutrition app. Personalized diet plans. 30 million+ users.

**Niramai, SigTuple:** AI-powered diagnostic tools. Niramai uses thermal imaging for breast cancer screening — more affordable and more accessible than mammography.

### Hospital Tech

**Practo:** Appointment booking, health records, clinic management software. Struggled to monetize the free consumer side.

## AgriTech — Feeding a Billion with Technology

India has 150 million+ farm families. Agriculture contributes 18% of GDP. Productivity is far below global standards. Technology is deeply underused.

The opportunity areas:

**Market linkage (connecting farmers to buyers):**
- **Ninjacart (acquired by BigBasket/Tata):** Direct farm-to-retailer supply chain for vegetables and fruits
- **AgriBazaar, NCDEX:** Online commodity trading platforms

**Credit and insurance:**
- **Samunnati, Jai Kisan:** Agricultural credit to farmers who can't access traditional banks
- Crop insurance against weather events

**Advisory:**
- **DeHaat, Fasal, Cropin:** Farm management apps — soil testing, weather alerts, disease detection, crop advisory

**Input supply:**
- **Bijak, Gramophone:** Online platform for seeds, fertilizers, pesticides

The challenge: India's farmers are fragmented (average farm size <2 acres), often not smartphone-native, and operate in cash. Technology must work through intermediaries (FPOs, mandis, input dealers) rather than direct to farmer.

## LogiTech — The Invisible Infrastructure

Every product sold online needs to be delivered. India's logistics infrastructure has transformed in 10 years.

**Delhivery:** India's largest logistics company. Built the supply chain infrastructure for e-commerce — express delivery, B2B freight, supply chain management. IPO'd in 2022.

**Shiprocket:** Logistics aggregator for D2C brands and SMBs. Integrates 25+ couriers, handles returns, tracking.

**Porter:** Intra-city logistics (like Ola for trucks). Moving goods within cities for small businesses.

**DTDC, Shadowfax:** Last-mile delivery specialists.

The logistics sector enables everything else. Without reliable, affordable delivery, e-commerce doesn't work. India's logistics cost as % of GDP (13-14%) is much higher than developed countries (8-9%) — the opportunity to reduce this through technology and operations is enormous.

## How to Identify the Next New Business Model

New business models emerge when:
1. New technology makes something possible that wasn't before (smartphones → gig economy)
2. Regulation changes to allow new activities (SEBI allowing mutual funds online → WealthTech)
3. New infrastructure arrives (UPI → fintech explosion)
4. Demographics shift (a generation grows up with different expectations)
5. A crisis forces behavior change (COVID → telemedicine, edtech)

**The next waves:**
- **AI agents replacing knowledge workers:** AI that can draft contracts, diagnose medical images, analyze financials
- **EV ecosystem:** Charging infrastructure, battery swapping, EV insurance, used EV marketplaces
- **Climate finance:** Carbon markets, green bonds, net-zero advisory for corporates
- **Bharat-focused internet:** Products for 700 million+ tier-2/3/rural Indians being onboarded
- **Semiconductor and advanced manufacturing:** India becoming a manufacturing destination

## Summary

- Fintech is India's most successful startup sector, uniquely enabled by UPI, Aadhaar, and India Stack
- Payments (Razorpay, PhonePe), lending (KreditBee, Bajaj Finance), wealth (Zerodha, Groww), insurance (Acko, Digit) — all have real businesses
- Edtech boomed in COVID and collapsed after — BYJU'S is the cautionary tale of growth-at-any-cost
- HealthTech and AgriTech have enormous markets but face distribution challenges in India's fragmented landscape
- New business models emerge from: new tech, new regulation, new infrastructure, demographic shifts
