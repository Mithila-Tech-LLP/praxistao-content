# Chapter 42: SaaS — Software as a Service, the Business Model That Changed Tech

*SaaS is the most investor-loved business model of the last 20 years. Here's why — and what it takes to build one.*

---

## 1. What SaaS Is and Why It's Revolutionary

Before SaaS, software was sold as a product. You bought Microsoft Office for ₹15,000, installed it on your computer, and used it for 3 years. Microsoft got paid once.

SaaS changed this: instead of buying software, you rent it. You pay ₹500/month for Google Workspace and access it through a browser. The software lives in the cloud. Updates are automatic. You never install anything.

For customers: no upfront cost, always up-to-date, accessible from anywhere.

For businesses: **predictable, recurring revenue** that grows over time without equivalent growth in costs.

That last point is why investors love SaaS. A ₹10 lakh/month customer in January becomes ₹10 lakh/month in February, March, and beyond — unless they cancel. Revenue is sticky.

---

## 2. The SaaS Revenue Model — Why Investors Love It

**Recurring Revenue:** Unlike a product sale that happens once, SaaS revenue repeats monthly or annually. A SaaS company with 100 customers paying ₹10,000/month has ₹10 lakh in predictable monthly revenue.

**Low Marginal Cost:** Serving customer #1,001 costs almost the same as customer #1,000. You've already built the software. The incremental cost is server capacity and support — both scale much more slowly than revenue.

**Compounding Effect:** Every month you retain existing customers AND add new ones, revenue compounds. A SaaS company growing 5% per month (60% per year) doubles in size every 14 months.

**High Gross Margins:** SaaS gross margins are typically 70-80%+. For every ₹100 in revenue, ₹70-80 is gross profit (before operating expenses). Compare this to e-commerce (20-30% gross margin) or food delivery (40-50% at best).

This combination — predictable, compounding, high-margin — makes SaaS the gold standard for investor returns.

## 3. The SaaS Metrics Deep Dive

*(Covered more fully in Chapter 34, but specific to SaaS here)*

**The Magic Number:**

LTV:CAC ratio must be greater than 3:1 for a sustainable SaaS business.

If customers pay ₹10,000/month and you have 80% gross margin and 2% monthly churn:
- LTV = (₹10,000 × 0.80) / 0.02 = ₹4,00,000

If CAC is ₹80,000:
- LTV:CAC = ₹4,00,000 / ₹80,000 = 5x ✓

If CAC is ₹2,00,000:
- LTV:CAC = ₹4,00,000 / ₹2,00,000 = 2x ✗ (not sustainable)

**NRR > 100% — The Magic Metric:**

If your existing customers expand their contracts (add seats, upgrade tiers, buy more) faster than churned customers reduce revenue, your NRR is above 100%.

A SaaS business with 120% NRR will grow its revenue from existing customers alone — even if it stops acquiring new customers. This is an extraordinary business property.

World-class SaaS: Snowflake (130%+ NRR), Twilio (120%+ at peak), Freshworks (~110%).

## 4. SMB SaaS vs Enterprise SaaS

The same product can serve different customers with radically different economics.

**SMB SaaS (Small and Medium Business):**
- Customers pay ₹1,000-20,000/month
- Self-serve onboarding (no sales calls)
- High volume, lower revenue per customer
- Faster sales cycle (days to weeks)
- Higher churn (SMBs go out of business, change direction more often)
- **Example:** Zoho Books (accounting), Freshdesk (customer support), Razorpay (payments)

**Enterprise SaaS:**
- Customers pay ₹1 lakh-1 crore+/month
- Requires a sales team, demos, security audits, procurement cycles
- Lower volume, much higher revenue per customer
- Long sales cycle (3-12 months)
- Lower churn (switching costs are high; procurement is painful)
- **Example:** Salesforce (CRM), ServiceNow (IT management), SAP (ERP)

The choice between SMB and enterprise is one of the most fundamental product and go-to-market decisions a SaaS founder makes.

## 5. PLG (Product-Led Growth) vs Sales-Led SaaS

**Sales-Led Growth (SLG):**
Traditional SaaS go-to-market. Marketing generates leads, sales qualifies and closes deals, customer success ensures retention.

Works for: Enterprise SaaS where customers need convincing, demos, and hand-holding.

**Product-Led Growth (PLG):**
The product itself drives acquisition, conversion, and expansion. Users find the product, try it for free, fall in love, and upgrade.

Works for: SMB/consumer SaaS where users can self-serve, and the product's value is immediately apparent.

**The hybrid (PLG + Sales):**
The best of both worlds. Users discover and try the product (PLG-driven). Sales teams follow up with high-potential accounts to convert and expand (Sales-driven). Slack, Figma, and Notion use this model.

## 6. SaaS Pricing Models

**Per Seat:** ₹500/user/month. The number of users determines cost. Simple to understand. Good for collaboration tools (everyone in the team is a user).

**Usage-Based:** Pay for what you use. Twilio charges per API call. AWS charges per compute hour. Great for developer tools; harder to forecast revenue.

**Flat Rate:** One price for unlimited everything. Simple but leaves money on the table with heavy users.

**Tiered:** Basic/Pro/Enterprise. Each tier has more features or users. The decoy effect (Chapter 15) works here — the middle tier is often most popular.

**Freemium:** Free tier with limited features, paid tier for more. Great for acquisition, requires careful thinking about what goes in free vs paid.

## 7. Indian SaaS Companies — Building for the World

India has produced some of the world's most successful SaaS companies. What makes this possible:

**Cost advantage:** Indian engineers cost 3-5x less than US counterparts. A SaaS company with $10M ARR can run leaner in India than the US.

**Time zone:** India can serve US customers from 9pm-5am IST, which aligns with US business hours.

**Technical excellence:** India's engineering talent (particularly IITs and NITs) is world-class.

### Freshworks

Founded by Girish Mathrubootham in Chennai in 2010. Started as a customer support tool (Freshdesk) for small businesses who couldn't afford Zendesk.

Grew to a multi-product suite: CRM, IT management, HR, sales. Listed on NASDAQ in 2021 at $9.3B valuation. First Indian SaaS company to list on a US exchange.

Revenue (2023): ~$600M ARR. Margin improvement ongoing.

### Zoho

The story of Zoho is one of the most remarkable in global SaaS. Sridhar Vembu built Zoho in Chennai with a philosophy of hiring from non-traditional educational backgrounds (local college graduates, sometimes school dropouts), paying well, and building for the long term.

Zoho now has 100+ products, competes with Salesforce, Google, Microsoft, and dozens of specialized players. Bootstrap. Profitable. 12,000+ employees.

**The Zoho philosophy:** Competition is good. Focus on building value. Don't raise money or optimize for exit. Think in 50-year horizons.

### Chargebee (Subscription Management)

Founded in Chennai, raised by Accel and Tiger Global, grew to ~$3.5B valuation as the subscription billing infrastructure for thousands of SaaS companies. Serves Freshworks, Calendly, Okta, and hundreds of others.

### Postman (API Development)

Founded in Bangalore by Abhinav Asthana and team. Became the standard tool for API development globally — 20 million+ developers use it. Valued at $5.6B. An Indian startup that became category-defining infrastructure for global software development.

## 8. The Path to ₹100 crore ARR

There is no single path, but here is a framework:

**Stage 1 (₹0 → ₹10 lakh ARR):** Get 10-50 paying customers. Entirely founder-led sales. Understand the problem deeply. Product is rough but core value is clear.

**Stage 2 (₹10 lakh → ₹1 crore ARR):** Find 2-3 repeatable acquisition channels. Hire first sales rep. Improve onboarding. Start building the customer success function.

**Stage 3 (₹1 crore → ₹10 crore ARR):** Scale acquisition. Reduce churn. Expand into adjacent customer segments. Raise Series A.

**Stage 4 (₹10 crore → ₹100 crore ARR):** Build sales organization. Launch new product modules. Enter enterprise segment. International expansion. Raise Series B.

At each stage, the constraint changes: finding customers → making them successful → scaling the machine → expanding the addressable market.

## 9. The Future of SaaS — AI-Native Products

AI is changing SaaS in fundamental ways:

**Copilot features:** Every SaaS product now has AI-powered assistance (write this email, summarize this contract, suggest this response). GitHub Copilot. Salesforce Einstein. Notion AI.

**Workflow automation:** AI agents that replace entire workflows, not just assist with them. Instead of paying for 10 seats of a CRM, a company pays for an AI that does the work of a sales team.

**Vertical AI:** AI trained on domain-specific data that outperforms generic LLMs. Legal AI (Harvey), medical AI (Suki, Nabla), financial AI.

**The threat to horizontal SaaS:** If AI can do the job that specialized software was doing, the software becomes less valuable. The companies that will survive are those whose moat is data, network effects, or customer relationships — not just feature richness.

---

## Summary

- SaaS = rent software instead of buying it; recurring, predictable, high-margin revenue
- Key metrics: MRR/ARR, churn, NRR (>100% is magic), LTV:CAC (>3x required)
- SMB SaaS = high volume, self-serve, faster churn; Enterprise SaaS = lower volume, sales-heavy, stickier
- PLG + Sales is the winning model for most modern SaaS
- India's SaaS story (Freshworks, Zoho, Chargebee, Postman) shows world-class companies built from Chennai and Bangalore
- AI is disrupting SaaS — the winners will have data moats and workflow ownership, not just features
