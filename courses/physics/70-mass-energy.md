## 1. What E = mc² Actually Means

Before Einstein, physicists thought mass and energy were completely separate, unrelated quantities. Mass was the "stuff" that made up matter — the inertia, the weight, the substance. Energy was something a moving object had, or what you stored in a spring or a chemical bond. The two lived in different compartments of physics.

Einstein's special theory of relativity (1905) demolished this partition with a single, devastating insight:

**Mass and energy are not separate things. They are two forms of the same thing.**

The equation **E = mc²** (more precisely **E₀ = m₀c²** for a stationary object) expresses this directly:

```
E₀  =  m₀  ×  c²
 |       |      |
Rest   Rest   Speed of light
energy  mass   squared
```

Think of it this way:

- **Mass is "frozen" energy.** When you hold a lump of matter in your hand, you are holding an unimaginable amount of stored energy in an incredibly compact form. The atoms are not vibrating rapidly, no chemical reactions are happening — and yet the sheer fact that matter *exists* means it contains energy.

- **Energy is "released" mass.** When energy flows out of a system — heat radiated from a fire, light emitted by a bulb, or the kinetic energy of explosion debris — the system's total *mass* decreases by a tiny amount.

The constant **c** is the speed of light in a vacuum:

```
c = 299,792,458 m/s  ≈  3 × 10⁸ m/s
```

And **c²** is therefore:

```
c² = (3 × 10⁸)²  =  9 × 10¹⁶  m²/s²
```

That number — **9 × 10¹⁶** — is 90 quadrillion. It is the "conversion factor" between kilograms and joules. Because c² is so enormous, even a tiny mass corresponds to a staggering amount of energy.

**Analogy:** Imagine mass as water frozen in a dam. The dam holds back an ocean of potential energy. In everyday life, the dam rarely breaks. But in nuclear reactions, a crack forms, and a tiny trickle of "frozen mass" converts into a flood of energy.

**The key insight:** In classical physics, mass was always conserved and energy was always conserved separately. In relativistic physics, they form a single conserved quantity — **mass-energy**. The total mass-energy of an isolated system never changes. But mass can convert to energy and energy can convert to mass.

## 2. Rest Energy — E₀ = m₀c²

**Rest energy** is the energy a stationary object has purely by virtue of having mass. It has nothing to do with motion, temperature, chemical state, or anything else. It is the irreducible, fundamental energy locked inside mass itself.

```
E₀ = m₀ × c²

where:
  E₀  = rest energy (in joules, J)
  m₀  = rest mass (in kilograms, kg)
  c   = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  c²  = 9 × 10¹⁶ m²/s²
```

### Calculation: 1 kg of any material

```
E₀ = 1 kg × (3 × 10⁸ m/s)²
E₀ = 1 × 9 × 10¹⁶ J
E₀ = 9 × 10¹⁶ J
```

**How much is that?**

- 1 ton of TNT releases about 4.2 × 10⁹ J
- So 9 × 10¹⁶ J equals about:

```
9 × 10¹⁶ / 4.2 × 10⁹  ≈  2.1 × 10⁷  tons of TNT
                        =  21 million tons of TNT
                        =  21 megatons of TNT
```

The **Tsar Bomba** — the largest nuclear weapon ever detonated (Soviet Union, 1961) — had a yield of about 50 megatons. So 1 kg of mass, if *completely* converted to energy, would be equivalent to roughly half the most powerful bomb ever built.

Note: In real nuclear weapons, only a tiny fraction of the mass actually converts to energy. The Tsar Bomba converted only about **2 kg** of matter into energy from a bomb containing thousands of kg of material. Complete conversion remains theoretical.

### Calculation: A single penny (2.5 g = 0.0025 kg)

```
E₀ = 0.0025 × 9 × 10¹⁶
E₀ = 2.25 × 10¹⁴ J
E₀ = 225 terajoules
```

The entire United States uses about 4 × 10¹⁹ J of energy per year. So a penny's worth of completely converted mass could power roughly:

```
2.25 × 10¹⁴ / (4 × 10¹⁹ / 3.15 × 10⁷)  ≈  1,770 seconds worth of US energy
```

That is around **30 minutes** of total US energy consumption from a 2.5-gram copper disc sitting in your pocket.

**Why don't we notice rest energy in everyday life?**

Because it is locked up. To access rest energy you need nuclear reactions or matter-antimatter annihilation. Chemical reactions — burning, exploding, metabolism — only rearrange electrons and bonds. They access a tiny, tiny fraction of rest energy. A wood fire converts mass to energy too, but the fraction is so small (~10⁻¹⁰ of total mass) it is completely undetectable with any practical scale.

## 3. Total Relativistic Energy — E = γm₀c²

When an object is moving, its total energy is greater than its rest energy. The **Lorentz factor γ** (gamma) accounts for relativistic effects:

```
         1
γ = ─────────────────
     sqrt(1 - v²/c²)
```

The total relativistic energy is:

```
E = γ × m₀ × c²
```

- When v = 0: γ = 1, so E = m₀c² (just rest energy)
- When v > 0: γ > 1, so E > m₀c² (rest energy PLUS kinetic contribution)
- When v → c: γ → ∞, so E → ∞

**Table of γ for various speeds:**

```
┌──────────────┬────────────────┬────────────────┐
│  Speed v     │  v/c ratio     │  γ (Lorentz)   │
├──────────────┼────────────────┼────────────────┤
│  0 m/s       │  0             │  1.000         │
│  300 km/s    │  0.001         │  1.000         │
│  30,000 km/s │  0.1           │  1.005         │
│  150,000 km/s│  0.5           │  1.155         │
│  240,000 km/s│  0.8           │  1.667         │
│  270,000 km/s│  0.9           │  2.294         │
│  297,000 km/s│  0.99          │  7.089         │
│  299,700 km/s│  0.9999        │  70.71         │
│  299,792 km/s│  0.99999       │  223.6         │
└──────────────┴────────────────┴────────────────┘
```

Notice how γ is nearly 1 at everyday speeds (even 300 km/s is a tiny fraction of c), but rockets upward as you approach the speed of light. This is why relativistic effects are invisible in ordinary life.

## 4. Relativistic Kinetic Energy and the Classical Limit

The **total energy** of a moving object is:

```
E = γm₀c²
```

The total energy is the sum of rest energy and kinetic energy:

```
E  =  E₀  +  KE
γm₀c²  =  m₀c²  +  KE

Therefore:
KE  =  (γ - 1) × m₀c²
```

This is the **relativistic kinetic energy** formula.

### Showing it reduces to ½mv² at low speeds

At low speeds (v << c), we can use the **binomial approximation**. Recall that for small x:

```
(1 - x)^(-1/2)  ≈  1 + x/2  +  (higher order terms)
```

Let x = v²/c²:

```
γ = (1 - v²/c²)^(-1/2)  ≈  1 + v²/(2c²)    (when v << c)
```

Substituting into the kinetic energy formula:

```
KE = (γ - 1) × m₀c²
   ≈ (1 + v²/(2c²) - 1) × m₀c²
   ≈ v²/(2c²) × m₀c²
   ≈ ½ × m₀ × v²
```

**This is exactly the classical kinetic energy!** Relativistic mechanics reduces perfectly to Newtonian mechanics at low speeds. This is the **correspondence principle** — any new theory must reproduce the old theory in the regime where the old theory works.

**Example: Comparing classical vs relativistic KE for a car at 100 km/h**

```
v = 100 km/h = 27.8 m/s
m₀ = 1000 kg
c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

Classical: KE = ½ × 1000 × (27.8)² = 386,420 J ≈ 386 kJ

Relativistic: KE = (γ - 1) × m₀c²
γ = 1 / sqrt(1 - (27.8)²/(3×10⁸)²)
  = 1 / sqrt(1 - 8.6×10⁻¹⁵)
  ≈ 1 + 4.3×10⁻¹⁵

KE = 4.3×10⁻¹⁵ × 1000 × (9×10¹⁶) = 386,000 J ≈ 386 kJ
```

The answers are identical for all practical purposes. The relativistic correction is about 10⁻¹⁵ — far beyond any measurement capability.

## 5. Energy-Momentum Relation — E² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)²

There is a powerful and beautiful relation connecting energy E, momentum p, and rest mass m₀:

```
E²  =  (p × c)²  +  (m₀ × c²)²
```

This is the **relativistic energy-momentum relation**. Think of it as a "Pythagorean theorem" in energy-momentum space.

```
       E
       |  \
       |    \
       |      \
  m₀c² |       \    E² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)²
       |        \
       └──────────
           pc
```

For a stationary object (p = 0):

```
E²  =  0  +  (m₀c²)²
E   =  m₀c²   ✓
```

This correctly gives the rest energy.

### Photons: Massless particles with momentum

A **photon** has no rest mass: m₀ = 0. Substituting:

```
E²  =  (pc)²  +  0
E   =  p × c
p   =  E / c
```

But we also know from quantum mechanics that E = hf (Planck's relation), so:

```
p  =  E/c  =  hf/c  =  h/λ
```

This is the **de Broglie / photon momentum formula**. Light carries momentum despite having no mass. This is not a contradiction of E = mc² — it is a consequence of the fuller relativistic picture. A photon's energy IS its momentum times c.

**Why this matters:** Solar sails, radiation pressure, optical tweezers — all these technologies exploit the momentum of light, which would be impossible to understand without the energy-momentum relation.

## 6. Why You Can Never Reach the Speed of Light

This is one of the most important — and most misunderstood — results of special relativity.

The total energy of a massive object is:

```
E  =  γ × m₀ × c²
```

As v approaches c, γ approaches infinity. Therefore E approaches infinity. To accelerate an object with mass to the speed of light would require **infinite energy**. Since the universe contains only finite energy, this is impossible.

**Intuitive picture:**

```
v/c:   0.0    0.5    0.9    0.99   0.999  0.9999  1.0
γ:     1.00   1.15   2.29   7.09   22.4   70.7    ∞
```

Each step toward c requires exponentially more energy. You approach c but never reach it — like a mathematical asymptote.

### Energy required to accelerate 1 kg to various speeds

```
┌──────────────┬────────┬────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┐
│  Speed (v/c) │  γ     │  Total Energy E (J)│  Kinetic Energy KE (J)   │
├──────────────┼────────┼────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┤
│  0.1         │  1.005 │  9.045 × 10¹⁶      │  4.5 × 10¹⁴              │
│  0.5         │  1.155 │  1.039 × 10¹⁷      │  1.39 × 10¹⁶             │
│  0.9         │  2.294 │  2.064 × 10¹⁷      │  1.164 × 10¹⁷            │
│  0.99        │  7.089 │  6.38  × 10¹⁷      │  5.48 × 10¹⁷             │
│  0.999       │  22.4  │  2.01  × 10¹⁸      │  1.92 × 10¹⁸             │
│  0.9999      │  70.7  │  6.36  × 10¹⁸      │  6.27 × 10¹⁸             │
│  1.0         │  ∞     │  ∞                 │  ∞                       │
└──────────────┴────────┴────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┘
```

To get 1 kg to 99.99% of the speed of light requires ~6.27 × 10¹⁸ J — about 70 times the rest energy of the object. The energy requirement grows without bound.

**Only massless particles** — photons, gluons, (possibly) gravitons — can travel at exactly c, because for them E = pc applies (they do not need to be "accelerated" to c; they are born at c and always travel at c).

## 7. Mass Defect and Nuclear Binding Energy

Here is one of the most surprising facts in all of physics: **a nucleus weighs less than the sum of its parts.**

Let us look at helium-4 (He-4), which has 2 protons and 2 neutrons.

```
Measured masses:
  Proton mass   =  1.007276 u
  Neutron mass  =  1.008665 u

Expected mass of He-4 = 2 × proton + 2 × neutron
                      = 2 × 1.007276  +  2 × 1.008665
                      = 2.014552  +  2.017330
                      = 4.031882 u

Actual measured mass of He-4 nucleus = 4.001506 u

Mass defect:
  Δm = 4.031882 - 4.001506 = 0.030376 u
```

The nucleus is **missing** 0.030376 u of mass. Where did it go?

It was converted into **binding energy** — the energy that holds the nucleus together. This energy was released when the nucleus formed. To pull the nucleus apart again, you must supply this exact amount of energy back.

```
Binding Energy  =  Δm × c²

Using: 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²  (this is a useful conversion unit)

BE(He-4)  =  0.030376 u × 931.5 MeV/u
           =  28.3 MeV
```

This means you must supply 28.3 MeV to completely disassemble a helium-4 nucleus into its 4 separate nucleons. That is the energy locked into the binding.

**The u (atomic mass unit):** 1 u = 1.66054 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. It is defined as 1/12 the mass of a carbon-12 atom. The conversion **1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²** is one of the most useful numbers in nuclear physics.

**Analogy:** Think of building a house. When workers assemble the house, they release energy (gravitational potential energy decreases). To disassemble the house back into individual bricks and beams, you must supply that energy back. The "assembled house" weighs very slightly less than the sum of all its loose components — because some mass was converted to the energy released during assembly. Nuclear binding is the same principle, but on a vastly more energetic scale.

## 8. Binding Energy Per Nucleon — The Curve That Rules Nuclear Physics

Instead of asking how much binding energy a whole nucleus has, it is more informative to ask: **how tightly bound is each individual nucleon (proton or neutron)?**

```
Binding energy per nucleon  =  Total BE / Number of nucleons (A)
```

This quantity, plotted against mass number A, produces the most important graph in nuclear physics:

```
BINDING ENERGY PER NUCLEON vs MASS NUMBER

(MeV)

    |               ****             ****

  7 |       *                                      *****
    |      *                                            ****
  6 |     *                                                 ****
    |    *                                                      ***
  5 |   *                                                 U-235 -->
    |  *
  4 |  *
    | *  Li-6, Be-9
  3 | *
    |*
  2 |H-2 (Deuterium, ~1.1 MeV)
    |
  1 |
    |
  0 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    H   He Li Be  C  O  Ne  Mg  Si  Fe         Sn       Au  U
    1   4  7  9  12  16  20  24  28  56         120      197 238
                        Mass Number A  →
```

**Reading the curve:**

- **Hydrogen-1 (single proton):** BE/A = 0 (nothing to bind)
- **Deuterium (H-2):** BE/A ≈ 1.1 MeV (loosely bound)
- **Helium-4:** BE/A ≈ 7.1 MeV (unusually stable due to magic numbers — there is a local bump)
- **Iron-56:** BE/A ≈ 8.8 MeV — **THE PEAK**. Iron is the most tightly bound nucleus. It is the most stable nucleus in the universe.
- **Uranium-238:** BE/A ≈ 7.6 MeV (less tightly bound than iron)

**The single most important thing this curve tells us:**

Nuclear reactions that move nuclei from LOWER on the curve to HIGHER on the curve RELEASE energy. Nuclear reactions going the other way REQUIRE energy.

```
FUSION direction:  Light nuclei → Heavier nuclei → moves UP the curve (left side)
                   Releases energy if product is higher than reactants

FISSION direction: Heavy nuclei → Medium nuclei  → moves UP the curve (right side)
                   Releases energy if products are higher than reactant

IRON: Cannot release energy from either fusion or fission.
      It is already at the top of the curve. The endpoint of nuclear energy.
```

This is why the cores of dying massive stars fill with iron and collapse — the iron cannot burn, it cannot release more energy to support the star against gravity. The core collapses into a neutron star or black hole.

## 9. Nuclear Fission

**Nuclear fission** is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into two (or sometimes three) smaller nuclei, with the release of neutrons and a large amount of energy.

The most important fissile materials are **uranium-235** and **plutonium-239**.

### The fission reaction of U-235

A slow (thermal) neutron is absorbed by U-235. The resulting U-236 nucleus is highly unstable and immediately splits. One common reaction:

```
U-235 + neutron  →  Ba-141 + Kr-92 + 3 neutrons + energy

(Uranium-235 + n  →  Barium-141 + Krypton-92 + 3n + ~200 MeV)
```

Those 3 neutrons can each trigger another fission — this is the **chain reaction**.

### ASCII diagram: Chain reaction

```
         n
          \
           [U-235]  →  [Ba] + [Kr] + n + n + n
                                      |    |    |
                                      |    |    |
                               [U-235]  [U-235]  [U-235]
                               → 3n    → 3n      → 3n
                              /  |  \   ...       ...
                             ...        (each generation multiplies)

UNCONTROLLED:  neutrons multiply exponentially  →  BOMB (microseconds)

CONTROLLED:    neutron-absorbing control rods inserted  →  REACTOR
               exactly 1 neutron per fission causes the next fission
               steady state  →  heat  →  steam  →  electricity
```

### Worked Example: Energy from 1 kg of U-235

Each fission of U-235 releases approximately **200 MeV**.

**Step 1: How many atoms in 1 kg of U-235?**

Molar mass of U-235 = 235 g/mol = 0.235 kg/mol

```
Number of moles = 1 kg / 0.235 kg/mol = 4.255 mol
Number of atoms = 4.255 × 6.022 × 10²³
               = 2.562 × 10²⁴ atoms
```

**Step 2: Total energy in MeV**

```
Total energy = 2.562 × 10²⁴ × 200 MeV
             = 5.124 × 10²⁶ MeV
```

**Step 3: Convert to joules (1 MeV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J)**

```
Total energy = 5.124 × 10²⁶ × 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J
             = 8.21 × 10¹³ J
             = 82.1 terajoules
```

**Step 4: Compare to TNT (1 ton TNT = 4.2 × 10⁹ J)**

```
Equivalent TNT = 8.21 × 10¹³ / 4.2 × 10⁹ = 19,500 tons of TNT
              ≈ 20 kilotons of TNT
```

This is roughly the yield of the Hiroshima bomb (Little Boy, ~15 kilotons). In that bomb, only about **0.7 kg** of uranium actually underwent fission out of ~64 kg total. The rest was blown apart before it could react.

**Nuclear power station context:** A 1-GW nuclear power plant uses about 27 tonnes of uranium fuel per year. A coal plant of the same power burns about 3 million tonnes of coal per year. The energy density advantage is roughly **100,000:1**.

## 10. Nuclear Fusion

**Nuclear fusion** is the combining of light nuclei into heavier nuclei, releasing energy when the product sits higher on the binding energy per nucleon curve.

The most energy-dense fusion reaction uses **deuterium** (H-2, one proton + one neutron) and **tritium** (H-3, one proton + two neutrons):

```
D + T  →  He-4 + n + 17.6 MeV

²H + ³H  →  ⁴He + ¹n + 17.6 MeV
```

### Why fusion is hard

Both deuterium and tritium are positively charged nuclei. To fuse, they must get close enough for the **strong nuclear force** to take over from the **Coulomb (electrical) repulsion**. This requires approaching within ~10⁻¹⁵ meters, which only happens at temperatures of roughly **100 million Kelvin** (about 7× hotter than the Sun's core — the Sun can get away with cooler temperatures because its immense gravity confines the plasma for billions of years).

At these temperatures, matter exists as a **plasma** — a soup of free electrons and nuclei. Confining this plasma is the central challenge of fusion energy research.

**Approaches to fusion:**

- **Magnetic confinement** — use powerful magnetic fields to contain the plasma in a donut-shaped chamber (**tokamak**). The ITER project in France is building the world's largest tokamak, targeting the first net-energy fusion reaction.
- **Inertial confinement** — use powerful lasers to compress and heat a tiny pellet of D-T fuel until it ignites (similar in principle to a hydrogen bomb, but controlled). The National Ignition Facility (NIF) in California achieved fusion ignition in 2022.

**Fusion vs Fission energy yield per kg:**

```
Fission (U-235):   ~82 TJ/kg  (from above)
Fusion (D-T):      ~340 TJ/kg
```

Fusion releases roughly **4 times more energy per kilogram** than fission. Deuterium is found in ordinary seawater (roughly 1 in 6,400 hydrogen atoms is deuterium). The energy in 1 liter of seawater, if its deuterium were fused, equals burning roughly 300 liters of gasoline.

**Hydrogen bomb:** Uses a fission bomb to create the extreme temperature and pressure needed to trigger fusion. The enormous energy release comes primarily from the fusion stage. This is why hydrogen bombs (thermonuclear weapons) are far more powerful than pure fission bombs.

## 11. Why the Sun Shines — The Proton-Proton Chain

The Sun is a giant nuclear fusion reactor. In its core, where the temperature is ~15 million K and the pressure is 250 billion atmospheres, hydrogen nuclei (protons) fuse together through a series of steps called the **proton-proton (pp) chain**.

### The overall reaction

```
4 ¹H  →  ⁴He  +  2 e⁺  +  2 νₑ  +  energy

(4 protons  →  1 helium-4 nucleus + 2 positrons + 2 electron neutrinos + energy)
```

The positrons immediately annihilate with electrons, releasing additional gamma radiation.

### Mass calculation

```
Mass of 4 protons:
  m(4 × ¹H)  =  4 × 1.007825 u  =  4.031300 u

  (Note: we use atomic mass of hydrogen, 1.007825 u, which includes
   the electron mass — this is standard for nuclear mass calculations)

Mass of He-4 atom:
  m(⁴He)  =  4.002602 u

Mass defect:
  Δm  =  4.031300 - 4.002602  =  0.028698 u

Energy released per reaction:
  E  =  0.028698 u × 931.5 MeV/u
     =  26.73 MeV

(The two positrons annihilate releasing 2 × 0.511 MeV × 2 = 2.04 MeV
 Total per 4 protons fused ≈ 26.73 MeV)
```

**How much mass does the Sun lose per second?**

The Sun's total luminosity (power output) is:

```
L_sun = 3.846 × 10²⁶ W  (watts = joules per second)
```

Using E = mc², the mass converted per second:

```
m = E / c²  =  L / c²
  =  3.846 × 10²⁶ / (9 × 10¹⁶)
  =  4.27 × 10⁹ kg/s
  ≈  4.3 million tonnes per second
```

**The Sun converts approximately 4 million tonnes of mass into pure energy every second.** It has been doing this for 4.6 billion years and has enough hydrogen to continue for another ~5 billion years. The Sun's total mass is about 2 × 10³⁰ kg — so even at this rate, it will only convert about 0.034% of its mass over its entire lifetime.

**Life on Earth** is powered almost entirely by this mass-to-energy conversion happening 150 million kilometers away. Photosynthesis captures the photons. Fossil fuels are ancient photosynthesis stored. Wind and weather are driven by solar heating. The food you ate today traces back to a nuclear reaction in a star.

## 12. Worked Example 1 — Deuterium-Tritium Fusion

**Problem:** Calculate the energy released when deuterium and tritium fuse to form helium-4 and a neutron.

```
D + T  →  He-4 + n
²H + ³H  →  ⁴He + ¹n
```

**Given masses (atomic mass units):**
- Deuterium (²H): 2.01410 u
- Tritium (³H): 3.01605 u
- Helium-4 (⁴He): 4.00260 u
- Neutron (¹n): 1.00867 u
- Conversion: 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Find total mass of reactants**

```
m_reactants  =  m(D)  +  m(T)
             =  2.01410  +  3.01605
             =  5.03015 u
```

**Step 2: Find total mass of products**

```
m_products  =  m(He-4)  +  m(n)
            =  4.00260  +  1.00867
            =  5.01127 u
```

**Step 3: Calculate mass defect**

```
Δm  =  m_reactants  -  m_products
    =  5.03015  -  5.01127
    =  0.01888 u
```

**Step 4: Convert mass defect to energy**

```
E  =  Δm × c²
   =  0.01888 u × 931.5 MeV/u
   =  17.59 MeV
   ≈  17.6 MeV
```

**Step 5: Convert to joules**

```
E  =  17.6 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J
   =  2.82 × 10⁻¹² J
```

**Interpretation:** Each single D-T fusion event releases 17.6 MeV, or 2.82 × 10⁻¹² J. This seems tiny, but consider that 1 gram of D-T fuel contains on the order of 10²³ such reactions.

**Energy per kg of D-T fuel:**

```
Molar mass of D+T mix ≈ 5 g/mol  (roughly)
Moles per kg = 1000 g / 5 g/mol = 200 mol
Reactions per kg = 200 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10²⁶ reactions

Total energy = 1.204 × 10²⁶ × 2.82 × 10⁻¹²
             = 3.40 × 10¹⁴ J
             = 340 terajoules per kg
```

This confirms the figure stated earlier — fusion D-T yields ~340 TJ/kg.

## 13. Worked Example 2 — Mass Converted in a Nuclear Reaction

**Problem:** A nuclear reaction releases 180 MeV of energy. How much mass was converted? Express your answer in kg and compare it to the mass of an electron.

**Given:**
- Energy released: E = 180 MeV
- 1 MeV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J
- c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
- Mass of electron: m_e = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Convert energy to joules**

```
E  =  180 MeV × 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J/MeV
   =  2.884 × 10⁻¹¹ J
```

**Step 2: Find mass from E = mc²**

```
m  =  E / c²
   =  2.884 × 10⁻¹¹ / (3 × 10⁸)²
   =  2.884 × 10⁻¹¹ / 9 × 10¹⁶
   =  3.20 × 10⁻²⁸ kg
```

**Step 3: Compare to electron mass**

```
m / m_e  =  3.20 × 10⁻²⁸ / 9.109 × 10⁻³¹
          =  351
```

The mass converted is about **351 times the mass of a single electron** — roughly 0.35 proton masses. This is an extraordinarily tiny amount of mass, yet it produced enough energy (180 MeV) to knock loose many atomic electrons.

**Alternative: Express in atomic mass units**

```
m  =  3.20 × 10⁻²⁸ kg / 1.66054 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/u
   =  0.193 u
```

(Or directly: 180 MeV / 931.5 MeV per u = 0.193 u)

## 14. Pair Production and Annihilation

E = mc² is a two-way street. Mass converts to energy, and energy can convert back to mass. The clearest laboratory demonstrations of this are **pair production** and **pair annihilation**.

### Pair Production: Energy → Mass

When a high-energy photon (a gamma ray) passes close to an atomic nucleus, it can spontaneously convert into an **electron-positron pair**:

```
γ  →  e⁻  +  e⁺

(gamma ray  →  electron  +  positron)
```

**Minimum energy requirement:**

The photon must have at least enough energy to create the mass of both particles at rest:

```
E_min  =  2 × m_e × c²
        =  2 × 0.511 MeV
        =  1.022 MeV
```

If the photon has more than 1.022 MeV, the excess energy becomes kinetic energy of the electron and positron.

```
PAIR PRODUCTION DIAGRAM:

      gamma ray photon  (E ≥ 1.022 MeV)
             |
             |      ← nucleus nearby (required for momentum conservation)
             ↓
     ────────X────────
            / \
           /   \
     e⁻  ←/     \→  e⁺
   (electron)   (positron)
```

**The nucleus is required** because momentum must be conserved, and a photon carries momentum — the nucleus absorbs some recoil momentum.

The positron is the **antiparticle** of the electron: same mass (0.511 MeV/c²), but opposite electric charge (+1e instead of -1e).

### Pair Annihilation: Mass → Energy

When an electron meets a positron, they are mutually annihilated. Their entire rest mass converts to electromagnetic energy — two gamma photons emitted in opposite directions:

```
e⁻  +  e⁺  →  γ  +  γ

(electron + positron  →  two gamma photons)
```

```
ANNIHILATION DIAGRAM:

   e⁻  →          ←  e⁺
         ANNIHILATION
        ↗             ↘
    γ  (0.511 MeV)   γ  (0.511 MeV)
   (emitted in exactly opposite directions to conserve momentum)
```

Each photon carries exactly 0.511 MeV — the rest energy of one electron (or positron).

**Why two photons, not one?** A single photon cannot be created in this process without violating conservation of momentum. Two back-to-back photons have equal and opposite momenta that cancel to zero — matching the near-zero momentum of the annihilating pair at rest.

**Medical application: PET scans**

**Positron Emission Tomography (PET)** relies on this annihilation. A radioactive tracer (e.g., fluorine-18 attached to glucose) is injected into a patient. Cancer cells and active brain regions metabolize glucose rapidly, concentrating the tracer. The tracer emits positrons, which immediately annihilate with nearby electrons, producing two 0.511-MeV gamma photons flying in exactly opposite directions. Detectors surrounding the patient catch both photons simultaneously (a **coincidence event**) and by triangulating the origin points of thousands of such events, reconstruct a 3D image of metabolic activity in the body.

```
PET SCAN PRINCIPLE:

        Detector ring
     ╔══════════════╗
     ║              ║
     ║   e⁻ + e⁺   ║
     ║    →  γ γ ←  ║   detectors record coincident hits
     ║              ║
     ╚══════════════╝
     Computer reconstructs 3D map of all annihilation sites
     = map of metabolic activity = tumor location, brain activity, etc.
```

## 15. Antimatter — The Mirror Universe

Every fundamental particle has a corresponding **antiparticle** with the same mass but opposite quantum numbers (charge, lepton number, baryon number, etc.).

```
┌──────────────────┬────────────────┬───────────────┬──────────────┐
│  Particle        │  Symbol        │  Antiparticle │  Symbol      │
├──────────────────┼────────────────┼───────────────┼──────────────┤
│  Electron        │  e⁻            │  Positron     │  e⁺          │
│  Proton          │  p             │  Antiproton   │  p̄           │
│  Neutron         │  n             │  Antineutron  │  n̄           │
│  Neutrino (e)    │  νₑ            │  Antineutrino │  ν̄ₑ          │
│  Muon            │  μ⁻            │  Antimuon     │  μ⁺          │
└──────────────────┴────────────────┴───────────────┴──────────────┘
```

When a particle meets its antiparticle, they **annihilate completely** — 100% of their rest mass converts to energy. This makes antimatter conceptually the most efficient fuel imaginable: 1 gram of matter + 1 gram of antimatter → 1.8 × 10¹⁴ J (180 terajoules).

### Why there is matter in the universe (an unsolved problem)

The **Big Bang** should have produced equal amounts of matter and antimatter. If that were true, they would have annihilated each other completely, leaving nothing but photons — no atoms, no stars, no planets, no life.

Yet here we are, made of matter. Something created a tiny asymmetry: for every billion antimatter particles created, there were one billion and *one* matter particles. After annihilation, that single surviving matter particle per billion pairs is what makes up the entire observable universe.

The source of this **matter-antimatter asymmetry (baryon asymmetry)** is one of the greatest unsolved problems in physics. Some experiments (notably at CERN and Fermilab) search for differences in how matter and antimatter particles decay, trying to find the mechanism that tipped the balance.

### Why antimatter is not a practical fuel

1. **Annihilation on contact:** Antimatter cannot be stored in any conventional container, since it would annihilate on contact with the walls.
2. **Production cost:** Antiprotons are produced at CERN by firing protons at a target at high energy. The process is extraordinarily inefficient — producing 1 nanogram of antihydrogen requires roughly 10¹⁷ J of input energy (far more than the 9 × 10⁷ J you would get from annihilating it).
3. **Storage:** Antihydrogen must be trapped in magnetic "bottles" (Penning traps). CERN's ALPHA experiment trapped antihydrogen atoms for about 1000 seconds in 2011 — a record at the time.

Antimatter remains a research tool and a conceptual benchmark, not a near-term energy source.

## 16. Worked Example 3 — Tritium Beta Decay

**Problem:** Tritium (hydrogen-3) undergoes **beta-minus decay**:

```
³H  →  ³He  +  e⁻  +  ν̄ₑ

(Tritium  →  Helium-3  +  electron  +  antineutrino)
```

Calculate the **Q-value** (energy released) of this decay.

**Given atomic masses:**
- Tritium (³H): 3.01605 u
- Helium-3 (³He): 3.01493 u
- Electron (e⁻): 0.000549 u
- Neutrino mass: negligible (≈ 0)
- 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Understand the atomic mass convention**

Atomic masses include the orbital electrons. ³H atomic mass includes 1 electron. ³He atomic mass includes 2 electrons.

When ³H decays to ³He + e⁻, the ³He atom already accounts for 2 electrons in its atomic mass, but we are adding an extra electron from the beta decay. We need to be careful.

Using **atomic masses directly** (the standard shortcut):

```
Q  =  [m(³H)  -  m(³He)] × c²    (in atomic mass convention, electron masses cancel)

Let us verify:
  Parent: ³H atom (1 electron in atomic mass)
  Products: ³He atom (2 electrons in atomic mass) + e⁻

  Electron accounting:
    Parent has 1 orbital electron counted in m(³H)
    Product has 2 orbital electrons counted in m(³He) and 1 beta electron
    Total electrons on product side: 3
    But parent only had 1 → we are short 2 electrons

  Actually for beta-minus, the clean way:
    Q = [m_nucleus(³H) - m_nucleus(³He) - m_e] × c²
```

Using atomic masses with the standard method for beta-minus decay:

```
Q = [m_atomic(³H) - m_atomic(³He)] × c²
  = [3.01605 - 3.01493] × 931.5 MeV
  = 0.01112 × 931.5
  = 10.36 MeV
```

Wait — let us redo this carefully including the electron explicitly:

```
Nuclear masses (subtract electron masses from atomic masses):

  m_nucleus(³H)  = m_atomic(³H)  - 1 × m_e
                 = 3.01605 - 0.000549 = 3.015501 u

  m_nucleus(³He) = m_atomic(³He) - 2 × m_e
                 = 3.01493 - 2 × 0.000549 = 3.013832 u

Q-value:
  Q = [m_nucleus(³H) - m_nucleus(³He) - m_e] × c²
    = [3.015501 - 3.013832 - 0.000549] × 931.5
    = [0.001120] × 931.5
    = 1.043 MeV
```

**The correct Q-value for tritium beta decay is approximately 18.6 keV** (the standard textbook value). The accepted masses are slightly more precise:

Using precise values:
- m(³H) = 3.016049 u
- m(³He) = 3.016029 u

```
Q = [3.016049 - 3.016029] × 931.5 MeV/u
  = 0.000020 × 931.5
  = 0.01863 MeV
  = 18.63 keV
```

**Step 2: Interpret the result**

Q = 18.63 keV is the total kinetic energy shared between the electron and the antineutrino. Because the antineutrino carries away a variable fraction of this energy (neutrino energy is not fixed), the beta electron has a **continuous spectrum** of energies from 0 to 18.63 keV. This continuous spectrum was historically puzzling until Pauli proposed the neutrino in 1930.

**Tritium practical note:** Tritium (half-life: 12.3 years) is used in self-luminous devices (watch dials, emergency exit signs) where the beta particles excite a phosphor. It is also the fuel for hydrogen bombs and fusion reactors.

## 17. Worked Example 4 — Positron Emission from Carbon-11

**Problem:** Carbon-11 undergoes **beta-plus (positron) decay**:

```
¹¹C  →  ¹¹B  +  e⁺  +  νₑ

(Carbon-11  →  Boron-11  +  positron  +  neutrino)
```

Calculate the Q-value. This is the reaction exploited in PET scans.

**Given atomic masses:**
- Carbon-11 (¹¹C): 11.01143 u
- Boron-11 (¹¹B): 11.00931 u
- Positron (e⁺) = electron mass: 0.000549 u
- 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Note the special accounting for positron emission**

For **beta-plus (positron) emission**, the positron is created where there was none before. In the atomic mass convention:

- Parent atom ¹¹C has 6 electrons in its atomic mass
- Daughter atom ¹¹B has 5 electrons in its atomic mass
- We also emit 1 positron (mass = m_e)

So effectively the atomic mass of the parent has 1 extra electron compared to the daughter, AND we must account for the positron's creation (which costs m_e × c²).

**Standard formula for beta-plus Q-value:**

```
Q = [m_atomic(parent) - m_atomic(daughter) - 2 × m_e] × c²
```

The factor of 2m_e appears because: the daughter has one fewer orbital electron (loses m_e compared to parent), AND we create a positron (costs another m_e).

**Step 2: Calculate**

```
Q = [m(¹¹C) - m(¹¹B) - 2 × m_e] × c²
  = [11.01143 - 11.00931 - 2 × 0.000549] × 931.5 MeV
  = [11.01143 - 11.00931 - 0.001098] × 931.5
  = [0.00102] × 931.5
  = 0.9502 MeV
  ≈ 0.96 MeV
```

**Step 3: Check for positron emission viability**

Positron emission can only occur if Q > 0:

```
Q = 0.96 MeV > 0  ✓  (positron emission is energetically allowed)
```

The condition Q > 0 requires:

```
m_atomic(parent) > m_atomic(daughter) + 2m_e
```

In other words, the parent must be heavier than the daughter by at least 2 electron masses (1.022 MeV/c²) for positron emission to occur. If the mass difference is less than 2m_e but still positive, the decay proceeds via **electron capture** (the nucleus grabs one of its own orbital electrons) instead.

**Step 4: Interpret for PET scans**

The emitted positron (0.96 MeV of available kinetic energy shared with the neutrino) travels only 1-2 mm in tissue before being annihilated by an electron:

```
e⁺ + e⁻ → γ + γ  (two 0.511 MeV photons, back-to-back)
```

PET detectors pick up these coincident photon pairs. The 0.511 MeV energy is a clear signature — PET scanners are tuned specifically to this energy.

**Carbon-11 in PET:** C-11 has a half-life of only 20.4 minutes. This is both a challenge (must be produced at the hospital in an onsite cyclotron and used immediately) and an advantage (the patient's radiation dose is low because the isotope decays away quickly).

## Summary

- **E = mc²** states that mass and energy are equivalent forms of the same physical quantity, related by the factor c² ≈ 9 × 10¹⁶ m²/s².

- **Rest energy** E₀ = m₀c² is the energy inherent in any mass at rest. 1 kg contains ~9 × 10¹⁶ J (equivalent to ~21 megatons of TNT).

- **Total relativistic energy** E = γm₀c² includes both rest energy and kinetic energy. The Lorentz factor γ = 1/sqrt(1 - v²/c²) grows without bound as v → c.

- **Relativistic kinetic energy** KE = (γ - 1)m₀c² reduces to the classical ½mv² at low speeds via the binomial approximation, confirming consistency with Newtonian mechanics.

- **Energy-momentum relation** E² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)² connects energy, momentum, and rest mass. For photons (m₀ = 0): E = pc, giving light its momentum despite being massless.

- **Speed of light is unbreachable** for massive objects: accelerating to c would require infinite energy as γ → ∞.

- **Mass defect** is the difference between the mass of a nucleus and the sum of its constituent protons and neutrons. This missing mass equals the **binding energy** BE = Δm × c² that holds the nucleus together.

- **Binding energy per nucleon** peaks at iron-56 (~8.8 MeV/nucleon). Reactions moving nuclei up this curve (toward iron) release energy. This underpins all nuclear energy.

- **Nuclear fission** splits heavy nuclei (U-235, Pu-239), releasing ~200 MeV per event. A chain reaction is the basis of both nuclear reactors and atomic bombs.

- **Nuclear fusion** joins light nuclei (D + T → He-4 + n, releasing 17.6 MeV) and yields ~4× more energy per kg than fission. It powers stars and hydrogen bombs.

- **The Sun** converts ~4 million tonnes of mass to energy per second via the proton-proton chain, sustaining all life on Earth.

- **Pair production** (γ → e⁻ + e⁺) demonstrates energy → mass conversion; requires ≥ 1.022 MeV.

- **Pair annihilation** (e⁻ + e⁺ → γ + γ) demonstrates mass → energy conversion; each photon carries 0.511 MeV. Forms the basis of PET scanning.

- **Antimatter** is produced equally with matter in the Big Bang; the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry is an unsolved fundamental problem in physics.

- **Q-values** for nuclear decays are computed from mass differences; Q > 0 means energy is released. The factor of 2m_e in positron emission accounts for the creation of the positron alongside the electron lost from the daughter atom.
