## 1. What Is This Chapter About?

Newton's Third Law sounds so simple that people think they understand it after reading it once. Then they try to apply it and get completely confused.

"For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction." Fine. But then why does anything ever move? If every force is cancelled by an equal and opposite force, shouldn't everything just stay still forever?

And if a rocket pushes gas out the back with enormous force, and the gas pushes the rocket forward with an equally enormous force — don't those cancel out?

The answer is: **no, they do not cancel.** And the reason they do not cancel is the single most important thing in this entire chapter.

**Action-reaction pairs ALWAYS act on DIFFERENT objects.**

You cannot cancel forces that act on different objects. You can only cancel forces acting on the SAME object.

This chapter will make that crystal clear through multiple everyday examples, ASCII diagrams, and a famous physics paradox involving a horse and a cart.

By the end you will be able to:

- State Newton's Third Law precisely
- Identify the two forces in any action-reaction pair, including what type of force each is and which object each acts on
- Explain why the Earth doesn't visibly accelerate when you jump on it
- Resolve the Horse-and-Cart Paradox
- Explain rocket propulsion without mentioning "pushing off air"
- Correctly identify what is NOT a Newton's Third Law pair
- Solve problems involving two objects pushing on each other

## 2. Newton's Third Law — The Official Statement

Here is Newton's Third Law, stated precisely:

**"Whenever object A exerts a force on object B, object B simultaneously exerts a force on object A that is equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and of the same type."**

Or more compactly:

```
If A exerts force F on B,
then B exerts force -F on A.

(The minus sign indicates opposite direction.)
```

The shorthand version — "for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction" — is fine as a memory hook, but the words "action" and "reaction" can be misleading. They suggest one force causes the other, with a time delay. This is wrong.

**Both forces exist simultaneously.** The moment you push on a wall, the wall is already pushing back on your hand. There is no sequence — they appear together and disappear together.

## 3. The Action-Reaction Pair — The Most Important Rule

The two forces in Newton's Third Law always form an **action-reaction pair** (also called a Newton's Third Law pair, or a force pair).

The critical rule — the one that trips everyone up — is:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  The two forces in an action-reaction pair ALWAYS act on    │
│  DIFFERENT objects. NEVER on the same object.               │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Let's illustrate. You push a wall:

```
         Force BY you ON wall
    YOU ─────────────────────→ WALL
         ←──────────────────────
         Force BY wall ON you
```

- Force 1: You push the wall to the right
- Force 2: The wall pushes you to the left

Force 1 acts on the WALL. Force 2 acts on YOU. They are on different objects. That is why they do not cancel.

Your Free Body Diagram for the WALL only includes Force 1. Your Free Body Diagram for YOU only includes Force 2. You never put both forces on the same FBD.

```
FBD of WALL:                     FBD of YOU:
        →                                ←
  [Force from you]               [Force from wall]
     on the wall                     on you
```

If the wall is fixed (attached to Earth), the wall/Earth system is so massive that a = F/m ≈ 0 and it does not visibly move. If you are on a skateboard, YOU will roll backward because no friction holds you.

## 4. Properties of Action-Reaction Pairs

Every action-reaction pair has four properties. These are ALWAYS true — no exceptions:

### Property 1: Equal in Magnitude

The two forces always have exactly the same size. If you push the wall with 50 N, the wall pushes you with exactly 50 N. Not 49 N. Not 51 N. Exactly 50 N.

```
|F_A on B| = |F_B on A|     (magnitudes are equal)
```

### Property 2: Opposite in Direction

The forces always point in exactly opposite directions — 180° apart.

```
F_A on B  ──→        (pointing right)
F_B on A  ←──        (pointing left)
```

### Property 3: Same Type of Force

If Object A exerts a gravitational force on Object B, then Object B exerts a gravitational force (not some other type) on Object A. If the contact is a normal force, the reaction is also a normal force.

```
Types of forces:
  - Gravitational (gravity, weight)
  - Normal (contact, perpendicular to surface)
  - Friction (contact, parallel to surface)
  - Tension (through ropes and strings)
  - Electromagnetic (at molecular level — underpins most contact forces)
```

### Property 4: Simultaneous

Both forces exist at exactly the same moment. One does not cause the other. They arise together.

```
Summary table:

Property          | Value
------------------|---------------------------------------
Magnitude         | Equal (|F_12| = |F_21|)
Direction         | Opposite (antiparallel)
Type              | Same (both gravitational, or both normal, etc.)
Timing            | Simultaneous (no delay)
Objects acted on  | DIFFERENT objects (this is the key one)
```

## 5. Why Doesn't the Earth Visibly Move When You Jump?

This is one of the classic "but wait" questions in physics. Let's work through it carefully.

When you jump, your legs push DOWN on the Earth. By Newton's Third Law, the Earth pushes UP on you with an exactly equal force. That upward force is what launches you into the air.

So both forces are equal. The Earth pushes you upward with force F. You push the Earth downward with force F.

Why does the Earth not visibly accelerate downward while you fly upward?

**Apply Newton's Second Law to each object:**

For you (mass ≈ 70 kg, F ≈ 700 N from leg muscles):
```
a_you = F / m_you
a_you = 700 / 70
a_you = 10 m/s²   ← you go flying upward at 10 m/s²
```

For the Earth (mass = 6 × 10²⁴ kg, same force F = 700 N):
```
a_Earth = F / m_Earth
a_Earth = 700 / (6 × 10²⁴)
a_Earth ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻²² m/s²
```

That is 0.00000000000000000000012 m/s². The Earth DOES accelerate. Newton's Third Law says it must. But the number is so unimaginably small that no instrument in the world can measure it.

```
COMPARISON:
  Your acceleration when jumping:   ~10 m/s²
  Earth's acceleration:             ~10⁻²² m/s²
  Ratio:                            10²³ (hundred billion trillion times smaller)

Same force, wildly different accelerations — because the masses are wildly different.
The Third Law is obeyed. The Second Law explains why only you visibly move.
```

**The key insight:** Newton's Third Law guarantees equal forces. Newton's Second Law (a = F/m) explains why equal forces on very different masses produce very different accelerations.

## 6. Walking — Newton's Third Law in Your Every Step

Walking is powered entirely by Newton's Third Law. Without a reaction force from the ground, you would just spin your legs in place and go nowhere — like walking on ice in socks.

Here is what actually happens with every step:

```
STEP 1: Your foot pushes BACKWARD and DOWN on the ground

          YOU
         /|
        / |
       /  |      ← Your foot pushes the ground diagonally
      /   |         backward and down
 ___/________
   GROUND
         ↗
   Ground pushes
   you forward and up
   (reaction force)
```

```
STEP 2: Ground pushes YOU forward and upward (reaction)

           YOU
          ↗ |
         /  |
        /   |
       /    |
  ___/________
    GROUND
    ↙
 Ground experiences
 force backward and down
 (but Earth is massive → tiny acceleration)
```

**The action:** Your foot pushes the ground backward.
**The reaction:** The ground pushes your foot (and therefore you) forward.

This is why:
- **Walking on ice is hard.** Ice has very low friction. Your foot slips backward. The ground cannot exert a strong forward reaction force because there's no grip. The reaction force is tiny.
- **Walking in space (zero gravity, no surface)** is impossible without something to push against — there is no surface for the reaction.
- **Good shoes matter.** More friction → more force your foot can exert backward → larger forward reaction force → faster acceleration.

```
FORCE PAIR FOR WALKING:

Force 1: Foot pushes ground BACKWARD
  - Acts ON: Ground
  - Direction: Backward (and slightly down)

Force 2: Ground pushes foot FORWARD
  - Acts ON: You (your foot)
  - Direction: Forward (and slightly up)

These are the action-reaction pair.
```

## 7. Rockets in Space — Pure Newton Third Law

Rockets are the purest demonstration of Newton's Third Law because there is no air to "push against." This surprises many people who think a rocket works by "pushing off the air."

**How rockets actually work:**

The rocket engine burns fuel and forces hot exhaust gas out of the nozzle at extremely high speed. The gas exits backward. By Newton's Third Law, the gas pushes the rocket forward.

```
                  ROCKET
   ┌──────────────────────────────┐
   │  Fuel + Oxidiser             │
   │  Combustion chamber          │ ────→  Direction of travel
   │                              │
   └────────────┬─────────────────┘
                │
                │  Exhaust gases shoot out here at high speed
                ↓  (BACKWARD)
         [Hot gas expelled]

ACTION:    Rocket pushes gas BACKWARD
REACTION:  Gas pushes rocket FORWARD

These are Newton's Third Law pair.
```

**Detailed diagram:**

```
                    →→→ Rocket moves this way →→→

           ╔═══════════════════════════╗
           ║                           ║
   ←←←←←← ║  COMBUSTION CHAMBER       ║
   ←←←←←← ║  (gas expelled backward)  ║
   ←←←←←← ║                           ║
           ╚═══════════════════════════╝

   ←←← Gas exits at high speed (backward)
                                         Rocket thrust acts forward →→→

Newton's 3rd Law:
  Force ON gas (backward):     F_rocket_on_gas  →  to the left
  Force ON rocket (forward):   F_gas_on_rocket  →  to the right
  |F_rocket_on_gas| = |F_gas_on_rocket| exactly
```

**Why rockets work in space:**

In space there is no air to breathe, but there IS propellant (fuel + oxidiser) inside the rocket. The rocket carries its own reaction mass — the gas it expels. It does not need air. It does not need ground. It just needs mass to throw backward.

**This is why:**
- Rockets can operate in the vacuum of space
- Rockets get lighter as they burn fuel (less mass means more acceleration for the same thrust)
- Multi-stage rockets drop empty fuel tanks to reduce mass
- Jet engines (different — they suck in air and push it backward, using the Third Law with air as the reaction mass)

## 8. Swimming, Rowing, and Propellers

The same principle powers everything that moves through water or air.

**Swimming (freestyle stroke):**
```
  ACTION:    Your hand and arm push water BACKWARD
  REACTION:  Water pushes your hand and arm FORWARD

        ←← Your arm sweeps backward through water ←←
                          ↕  (Newton 3rd pair)
        →→ Water pushes you forward             →→
```

**Rowing:**
```
  ACTION:    Oar blade pushes water BACKWARD
  REACTION:  Water pushes oar (and boat) FORWARD
```

**Propeller (ship or plane):**
```
  ACTION:    Propeller blades push air/water BACKWARD
  REACTION:  Air/water pushes propeller FORWARD

  The propeller is shaped like a twisted wing — each blade pushes
  fluid backward as it spins.
```

**Bird wings:**
```
  ACTION:    Wing pushes air DOWNWARD
  REACTION:  Air pushes bird UPWARD (lift)

  ACTION:    Wing pushes air BACKWARD
  REACTION:  Air pushes bird FORWARD (thrust)
```

Notice the pattern: in all these cases, the object **throws mass in one direction** and gets pushed in the opposite direction. Same fundamental mechanism as a rocket — just using the surrounding fluid (air or water) as the reaction mass rather than carried propellant.

## 9. The Book on the Table — What Is NOT a Third Law Pair

This is one of the most important misconceptions to address. Students often mistake a balanced-force pair for a Newton's Third Law pair. They are NOT the same thing.

**The scenario:** A book sits at rest on a table.

Two forces act on the book:
1. Weight W = mg (gravity pulling the book DOWN)
2. Normal force N from the table (pushing the book UP)

These two forces are equal and opposite. But ARE they a Newton's Third Law pair?

**Answer: NO. They are NOT a Newton's Third Law pair.**

```
CORRECT IDENTIFICATION:

Forces on the BOOK:
  W = mg (down) — gravity from Earth on the book
  N (up)        — normal force from table on the book

These two are NOT a Newton's 3rd law pair because:
  → They both act on the SAME object (the book)
  → They are different TYPES of force (gravity vs contact)
  → They happen to be equal only because the book is in equilibrium

(If you added extra weight to the book, N would increase but W stays the same —
they are independent forces that happen to balance here.)
```

**What ARE the actual Newton's Third Law pairs here?**

For the gravitational force:
```
Force 1: Earth pulls book DOWN (gravity)
  Acts on: BOOK

Force 2: Book pulls Earth UP (gravity)
  Acts on: EARTH

These ARE a Newton's 3rd law pair:
  → Same type (both gravitational)
  → Equal and opposite
  → Act on DIFFERENT objects (book and Earth)
```

For the contact force:
```
Force 1: Table pushes book UP (normal force)
  Acts on: BOOK

Force 2: Book pushes table DOWN (normal force)
  Acts on: TABLE

These ARE a Newton's 3rd law pair:
  → Same type (both contact/normal)
  → Equal and opposite
  → Act on DIFFERENT objects (book and table)
```

```
FULL PICTURE:

         Book pulls Earth upward (gravity)
         ↑   ← tiny, unmeasurable
  ──── EARTH ──────────────────────────────────────────
                    │ Table pushes book up (N)
         ┌──────────┴────────┐
         │      TABLE        │ ← Book pushes table down (N)
         └──────────┬────────┘
                    │ Weight: Earth pulls book down (W)
               ┌────┴────┐
               │  BOOK   │
               └─────────┘

Newton's 3rd law pairs:
  Pair A: [Earth pulls book down] ↔ [Book pulls Earth up]
  Pair B: [Table pushes book up]  ↔ [Book pushes table down]

NOT a Newton's 3rd law pair:
  [Weight on book] and [Normal on book]
  (they both act on the same object — the book)
```

**The quick test for Newton's Third Law pairs:**
- Do the forces act on DIFFERENT objects? If yes, they could be a pair.
- Are they the same TYPE of force? If yes, they are likely a pair.
- Are they equal and opposite? If yes and both above conditions met, confirmed pair.

## 10. Gun Recoil — Same Force, Different Accelerations

When a gun fires a bullet, the explosion pushes the bullet forward and the gun backward simultaneously.

```
                BEFORE FIRING
         ┌──────────────────┐
         │  GUN  [bullet]   │
         └──────────────────┘
                Stationary

                DURING FIRING (explosion)
         ┌──────────────────┐
         │      GUN         │ ←──── F (backward force on gun)
         └──────────────────┘
                                 F (forward force on bullet) ────→ [•]

                AFTER FIRING
         ←── GUN moves slowly     BULLET ──────────────────→
                                  (moves very fast)
```

**ACTION:** Gun pushes bullet FORWARD with force F
**REACTION:** Bullet pushes gun BACKWARD with force F (same magnitude!)

But the gun is much heavier than the bullet. Newton's Second Law gives them very different accelerations:

```
Typical values:
  Force on bullet: F = 5000 N (for about 0.001 seconds)
  Force on gun:    F = 5000 N (same force, opposite direction)

  Mass of bullet:  m_bullet = 0.01 kg  (10 grams)
  Mass of gun:     m_gun    = 2.0 kg   (2000 grams)

Acceleration of bullet:
  a_bullet = F / m_bullet = 5000 / 0.01 = 500,000 m/s²   ← enormous!

Acceleration of gun:
  a_gun = F / m_gun = 5000 / 2.0 = 2,500 m/s²   ← much smaller

Ratio: the bullet accelerates 200× faster than the gun.
```

This is why the bullet shoots out at hundreds of metres per second while the gun only "kicks" back a small amount. Same force — but 200× the mass difference → 200× difference in acceleration.

**A heavier gun kicks less:** If you double the gun's mass, its recoil acceleration halves. This is why heavy military rifles have lower felt recoil than light guns with the same bullet. Heavier = harder to accelerate = gentler kick.

## 11. The Horse-and-Cart Paradox

This is perhaps the most famous conceptual puzzle in introductory physics. It goes like this:

> A horse is harnessed to a cart. By Newton's Third Law, the cart pulls back on the horse with a force equal to the horse's pull on the cart. Since these forces cancel, the horse can never accelerate the cart. So nothing ever moves. How do carts ever move?

This paradox has confused students for centuries. The resolution requires you to carefully track which forces act on which objects.

```
THE SETUP:

     ←─ Cart pulls horse backward (T)
  ┌──────┐                    ┌───────┐
  │HORSE │════════════════════│ CART  │
  └──────┘                    └───────┘
     ─→ Horse pulls cart forward (T)

This IS a Newton's 3rd law pair:
  Force 1: Horse pulls cart forward (T)   → acts on CART
  Force 2: Cart pulls horse backward (T)  → acts on HORSE

These act on DIFFERENT objects, so they do NOT cancel.
```

**The resolution:**

The paradox fails because it ignores the other forces. Let's draw proper FBDs.

**FBD of the CART:**

```
            N_cart
              ↑
              |
    T (horse) →[CART]← friction/resistance from ground and load
              |
              ↓
           W_cart

For the cart to accelerate:
ΣF_cart = T_horse_on_cart - F_resistance
If T > F_resistance, the cart accelerates.
```

**FBD of the HORSE:**

```
           N_horse
              ↑
              |
  F_ground →[HORSE]← T_cart_on_horse
              |
              ↓
           W_horse

For the horse to accelerate:
ΣF_horse = F_ground_on_horse - T_cart_on_horse
```

**The key force everyone forgets:** The ground pushes the horse forward!

When the horse pushes its hooves backward against the ground (action), the ground pushes the horse's hooves forward (reaction). This is the same mechanism as walking.

```
FULL PICTURE:

  ↑ N_horse            ↑ N_cart
  |                    |
  |     T ←→ T         |
[HORSE]──────────────[CART]
  |                    |
  ↓ W_horse            ↓ W_cart
  ↗ F_ground
(ground pushes horse forward)
```

**Force analysis:**

For the cart to start moving, the horse-on-cart tension T must exceed cart friction.

For the horse to move forward (and pull the cart), the ground-on-horse force must exceed the cart-on-horse tension T.

**The cart accelerates if:**
```
T > rolling resistance of cart

The horse+cart system accelerates if:
F_ground_on_horse > total friction (horse friction + cart friction)
```

The "equal and opposite" forces (horse-on-cart tension and cart-on-horse tension) act on DIFFERENT objects. They do not cancel each other. They affect the motion of their respective objects.

```
PARADOX RESOLUTION SUMMARY:

The mistake in the paradox is treating the horse-cart as one system
and saying the forces cancel. But:

1. The T forces are on DIFFERENT objects → cannot cancel each other.
2. What matters for the CART's motion is: T vs. cart resistance.
3. What matters for the HORSE's motion is: ground reaction vs. T.
4. The horse gets its forward force NOT from the cart, but from the GROUND.
5. The horse can generate a T that exceeds cart resistance → cart moves.
```

## 12. Contact Forces vs Field Forces — Both Obey Newton 3

Newton's Third Law applies to ALL types of forces — not just the ones you can see and touch.

**Contact forces** require the two objects to be physically touching:
- Normal force (surfaces pressing together)
- Friction (surfaces sliding)
- Tension (rope connecting objects)
- Air resistance (air and object touching)

**Field forces** act at a distance without contact:
- Gravity (Earth and Moon attract each other across 384,000 km of empty space)
- Magnetic forces (magnets attract/repel without touching)
- Electric forces (charged objects push/pull at a distance)

For gravity:
```
  Earth pulls Moon toward Earth (gravitational force)
  Moon pulls Earth toward Moon (gravitational force, equal and opposite)

  → Moon orbits Earth because Earth's gravity curves Moon's path
  → Earth also "wobbles" slightly because of Moon's gravity (this is
    how astronomers detect planets around other stars — they look for
    the tiny wobble the planet causes in the star's position)
```

For magnets:
```
  Magnet A attracts Magnet B toward A
  Magnet B attracts Magnet A toward B
  Equal, opposite, simultaneous — Newton's Third Law ✓
```

## 13. Identifying Newton's Third Law Pairs — A Systematic Method

Here is a step-by-step method to correctly identify Newton's Third Law pairs:

```
STEP 1: Identify a force.
  Name it as: "[Type of force] exerted by [Object A] on [Object B]"
  Example: "Normal force exerted by the TABLE on the BOOK"

STEP 2: Write the reaction pair.
  Swap the objects: "[Same type of force] exerted by [Object B] on [Object A]"
  Example: "Normal force exerted by the BOOK on the TABLE"

STEP 3: Verify:
  ✓ Same type of force? Yes (both normal/contact) ✓
  ✓ Equal magnitude? Yes ✓
  ✓ Opposite direction? Yes (one up, one down) ✓
  ✓ Different objects? Yes (table and book) ✓
```

**Practice Identification:**

```
Given force: Gravity from Earth on you (your weight, downward)
Reaction:    Gravity from you on Earth (upward, very tiny effect)
Same type?   Yes (gravitational)
Diff objects? Yes (Earth and you) ✓

Given force: You push a shopping cart forward
Reaction:    Cart pushes you backward
Same type?   Yes (contact/normal)
Diff objects? Yes (cart and you) ✓

Given force: Tyres push road backward (friction)
Reaction:    Road pushes tyres forward (friction)
Same type?   Yes (friction/contact)
Diff objects? Yes (road and tyres) ✓

Given force: Normal force from floor on your feet (upward)
Reaction:    Normal force from your feet on floor (downward)
Same type?   Yes (contact)
Diff objects? Yes (floor and you) ✓

NOT a pair: Weight on book (down) + Normal on book (up)
Why not?     Both act on the SAME object (the book) ✗
```

## 14. Worked Example: Two Ice Skaters Pushing Off

**Problem:** Two ice skaters stand facing each other on a frictionless ice rink. Skater A (mass = 60 kg) pushes off Skater B and moves away at 2 m/s. Skater B has mass 80 kg. Find Skater B's velocity after they push off.

**Setup:**

```
      BEFORE PUSH:
      Both at rest (v = 0)

         [Skater A: 60 kg] ←→ [Skater B: 80 kg]
         both stationary

      DURING PUSH:
      They push on each other simultaneously

         [A: 60 kg] ←F──────── F→ [B: 80 kg]
         (B pushes A left)   (A pushes B right)
         Equal forces, opposite directions (Newton 3rd Law)

      AFTER PUSH:
         ←── A moves left at 2 m/s        B moves right at v_B ──→
```

**Method using Newton's Third Law and Newton's Second Law:**

They interact for the same amount of time (the duration of the push, t).

Since F_A_on_B = F_B_on_A (Newton's Third Law):

```
F × t = F × t   (same force, same time)

This means:  m_A × a_A × t = m_B × a_B × t

Since a × t = change in velocity (from v = u + at, with u = 0):

m_A × v_A = m_B × v_B
```

(This is actually the **conservation of momentum**, which we will study fully in Chapter 17. Newton's Third Law is the reason momentum is conserved!)

Substituting values:

```
m_A × v_A = m_B × v_B
60 × 2 = 80 × v_B
120 = 80 × v_B
v_B = 120 / 80
v_B = 1.5 m/s
```

**Answer:** Skater B moves at **1.5 m/s** in the opposite direction to Skater A.

**Verification:** Skater A (60 kg) at 2 m/s. Skater B (80 kg) at 1.5 m/s.
- Skater A has 60 × 2 = 120 kg·m/s of momentum (to the left)
- Skater B has 80 × 1.5 = 120 kg·m/s of momentum (to the right)
- Total momentum: 120 - 120 = 0 (same as before push)

Total momentum is conserved — exactly as Newton's Third Law requires.

**Why does the lighter skater move faster?** Same force, less mass → greater acceleration → greater final velocity. (a = F/m, smaller m → larger a)

## 15. Why Forces Don't Cancel Even Though They Are Equal

Let's hammer this point home once more, because it is so commonly misunderstood.

**Forces cancel when they act on the SAME object and are equal and opposite.**

For example, if two people push you from opposite sides with equal force, the forces cancel — you do not accelerate.

```
Case 1: Forces on SAME object (they cancel):

  50 N ←─── YOU ───→ 50 N

  F_net = 50 - 50 = 0 N
  You don't accelerate.
  These are NOT a Newton's 3rd law pair (same object).
```

**Newton's Third Law pair forces NEVER act on the same object, so they NEVER cancel.**

```
Case 2: Newton's 3rd Law pair (different objects — they do NOT cancel):

          50 N               50 N
  YOU ────────────→ WALL ←────────── ?

  No. Newton's 3rd Law says:
  YOU pushes WALL at 50 N →
  WALL pushes YOU at 50 N ←

  For YOU's motion:  F_net on YOU = 50 N (backward push from wall)
  For WALL's motion: F_net on WALL = 50 N (forward push from you)

  They are on DIFFERENT objects. Each affects the object it's on.
```

```
RULE TO REMEMBER:

  "Equal and opposite" has TWO meanings in Newton's laws:

  Meaning 1 (Newton's 1st/2nd Law — equilibrium):
    Forces on the SAME object are equal and opposite → F_net = 0 → no acceleration
    These cancel each other.

  Meaning 2 (Newton's 3rd Law):
    Forces on DIFFERENT objects are equal and opposite → each affects its own object
    These do NOT cancel each other.

Never confuse these two!
```

## 16. Real World Applications

Newton's Third Law is not just a physics exercise — it explains and enables real technology.

**Space travel:** Rockets expel mass (exhaust gas) to generate thrust. The entire space programme depends on this. Without Newton's Third Law, there is no way to move in the vacuum of space.

**Jet propulsion:** Jet engines suck in air and expel it at much higher speed. The air going backward → plane goes forward.

**Helicopters:** Rotor blades push air downward → air pushes helicopter upward (lift). The tail rotor pushes air to one side → counteracts the spinning torque.

**Car tyres:** The engine turns the wheels, wheels push road backward, road pushes car forward. Four-wheel drive puts more "pushing wheels" in contact with the road.

**Sprinting:** Athletes push the starting blocks backward and downward. The blocks push the athlete forward and upward at the start of a race.

**Sailing:** A sail catches the wind (air pushes the sail forward). But a modern sailboat can also sail against the wind using the aerodynamic shape of the sail — the sail deflects air to one side, and the air pushes back on the sail (and boat) in the perpendicular direction, which — combined with the keel in the water — produces forward motion.

**Firearms and artillery:** All recoil is Newton's Third Law. Artillery cannons used to roll backward significantly after firing until engineers introduced recoil-dampening mechanisms.

## 17. Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Thinking Third Law Forces Cancel

The most common mistake. Equal and opposite does NOT mean they cancel — they act on DIFFERENT objects.

```
WRONG: "The cart pulls back on the horse equally, so the horse can't move the cart."
RIGHT: "The reaction force acts on the horse, not the cart. The cart's motion
        depends on the net force on the CART."
```

### Mistake 2: Confusing Equilibrium Pairs with Newton 3rd Law Pairs

Weight down and Normal force up on a stationary book are NOT a Newton 3rd Law pair. They both act on the book. They are equal only because the book happens to be in equilibrium.

### Mistake 3: Thinking One Force Causes the Other (With a Time Delay)

Both forces appear simultaneously. The word "reaction" suggests a response after the fact, but in physics they are truly simultaneous. The moment contact is made, both forces exist.

### Mistake 4: The Forces Being Equal Means Nothing Moves

Equal forces on DIFFERENT objects give different accelerations if the masses are different. A tiny bullet and a large gun experience equal forces but wildly different accelerations.

### Mistake 5: Forgetting Newton 3 Applies to Non-Contact Forces

Students remember Newton 3 for pushes and pulls but forget it applies to gravity, magnetism, and electric forces too.

## 18. Practice Problems

**Problem 1 (Conceptual)**
You push a 10 kg box to the right with 20 N. What force does the box exert on you, and in what direction? What object does it act on?

**Problem 2 (Conceptual)**
A 1 kg book sits on a shelf. List ALL the Newton's Third Law pairs involved. For each force, name: the type of force, which object exerts it, and which object receives it.

**Problem 3 (Conceptual)**
A space probe in deep space fires its engine for 10 seconds, expelling gas at high speed. Explain using Newton's Third Law why the probe accelerates, even though there is no air or ground.

**Problem 4 (Calculation)**
Two ice skaters push off each other. Skater X (mass 50 kg) reaches a velocity of 3 m/s. Skater Y has mass 75 kg. What velocity does Skater Y reach?

**Problem 5 (Conceptual)**
A horse is pulling a cart and accelerating. The cart pulls the horse backward with force T, and the horse pulls the cart forward with force T (same magnitude, Newton 3rd Law). Explain in detail how the horse manages to accelerate the cart.

**Problem 6 (Calculation)**
A gun (mass 1.5 kg) fires a bullet (mass 0.015 kg). The bullet leaves the barrel at 400 m/s. Find the recoil speed of the gun. (Hint: m_bullet × v_bullet = m_gun × v_gun)

---

**Answers:**

1. The box exerts 20 N on you, to the LEFT. It acts on YOU.

2. Newton 3 pairs:
   - [Earth pulls book down (gravity)] ↔ [Book pulls Earth up (gravity)]
   - [Shelf pushes book up (normal)] ↔ [Book pushes shelf down (normal)]
   NOT a pair: Weight on book and Normal on book (same object)

3. The engine expels gas backward (action force on gas). By Newton 3, the gas exerts an equal and opposite force on the probe forward. No air needed — the gas itself is the reaction mass.

4. m_X × v_X = m_Y × v_Y → 50 × 3 = 75 × v_Y → v_Y = 150/75 = **2 m/s**

5. The horse pushes the ground backward with its hooves. The ground pushes the horse forward (reaction). This forward ground-reaction force on the horse is what drives the horse (and therefore the cart) forward. The horse-cart tension T and the cart-horse tension T are a Newton 3 pair on different objects and do not cancel. The cart accelerates if T exceeds the cart's rolling friction.

6. m_bullet × v_bullet = m_gun × v_gun → 0.015 × 400 = 1.5 × v_gun → v_gun = 6/1.5 = **4 m/s** (backward)

## 19. Summary

- **Newton's Third Law** states: whenever A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal, opposite, and simultaneous force on A. They are always the same type of force.

- The two forces in an action-reaction pair ALWAYS act on **different objects**. This is the single most important point in the chapter.

- Because they act on different objects, Newton's Third Law forces never cancel each other. Each force affects the motion of the object it acts on.

- Forces on the SAME object cancel (or partially cancel) when they are equal and opposite — this is Newton's First/Second Law equilibrium, NOT Newton's Third Law.

- **Walking**: foot pushes ground backward → ground pushes foot (and you) forward.

- **Rockets**: engine pushes exhaust gas backward → gas pushes rocket forward. No air required.

- **The Earth does accelerate** when you jump on it — but its acceleration is undetectably tiny (a = F / m_Earth ≈ 10⁻²² m/s²) because its mass is enormous.

- **Gun recoil**: bullet and gun experience the same force magnitude, but different masses give very different accelerations. The light bullet accelerates much more than the heavy gun.

- **The Horse-and-Cart Paradox** is resolved by recognising the horse gets its forward force from the ground (not from the cart), and the horse-on-cart and cart-on-horse tension forces act on different objects.

- Newton's Third Law applies to ALL forces: contact forces (normal, friction, tension) and field forces (gravity, magnetism, electric).

- The method for identifying a Newton's Third Law pair: swap the two objects in the force description and check same type + different objects + equal + opposite + simultaneous.

- Two ice skaters pushing off illustrates how Newton's Third Law links to momentum conservation: m_A × v_A = m_B × v_B.

## 20. Key Equations and Rules

```
Newton's Third Law:
   If A exerts force F on B,
   then B exerts force -F on A  (equal magnitude, opposite direction)

   |F_A_on_B| = |F_B_on_A|   (magnitudes are equal)

Newton's Third Law Pair — ALL five of these must be true:
   ✓ Equal magnitude
   ✓ Opposite direction
   ✓ Same type of force
   ✓ Simultaneous
   ✓ Act on DIFFERENT objects

Why equal forces give different accelerations (Newton 2):
   a = F / m
   Same F, different m → different a

Skaters / momentum in Newton 3 collisions:
   m_A × v_A = m_B × v_B
   (derived from Newton 3: equal force, equal time → equal impulse)

Quick identification of Newton 3 pairs:
   Force: "[Type] by [Object A] on [Object B]"
   Pair:  "[Type] by [Object B] on [Object A]"

Forces that cancel = act on SAME object, equal and opposite (equilibrium)
Forces that don't cancel = Newton 3 pairs on DIFFERENT objects
```
