## 60.1 The Electromagnetic Spectrum

All electromagnetic waves travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum and follow:

```
c = f × λ

f = frequency (Hz)
λ = wavelength (m)
```

The **electromagnetic spectrum** arranges all EM waves by frequency/wavelength:

```
ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM

Low frequency          VISIBLE         High frequency
Long wavelength         LIGHT          Short wavelength
Low energy                             High energy

|           |           |    |    |           |           |
Radio    Microwave  Infrared  UV  X-ray   Gamma ray

Frequency (Hz):
10³      10⁹      10¹²   10¹⁵  10¹⁸    10²¹
                         (visible)

Wavelength:
1 km   0.3 m    0.3 mm  400nm  0.1nm   0.001nm
                         (visible)
```

Memory aid for visible spectrum: **ROY G BIV**
- Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet
- Red = longest wavelength (lowest frequency), Violet = shortest (highest frequency)

## 60.2 Radio Waves

**Frequency**: 3 Hz to 300 GHz (3×10⁸ Hz)
**Wavelength**: 1 mm to 10⁵ km
**Energy per photon**: very low (10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻³ eV)

### How They're Produced

Oscillating electrons in an antenna (transmitter).

```
RADIO TRANSMITTER:
  
  AC generator → antenna
  
  Electrons oscillate up and down in antenna at frequency f
  → produce EM wave at frequency f
  → wave propagates outward at speed c
  
  Receiver antenna: EM wave causes electrons to oscillate
  → picked up and decoded
```

### Sub-bands and Applications

| Band | Frequency | Application |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| AM Radio | 540–1600 kHz | Medium-wave broadcasting |
| FM Radio | 88–108 MHz | High-quality audio |
| VHF TV | 30–300 MHz | Old TV broadcasts |
| UHF TV | 300 MHz–3 GHz | Modern TV, 4G |
| WiFi | 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz | Wireless networks |

### Properties

- Can diffract around buildings and over hills (long wavelength diffraction)
- AM waves bounce off the ionosphere → long-range communication
- FM waves line-of-sight → limited range but higher quality
- Can pass through walls (why phones work indoors)

## 60.3 Microwaves

**Frequency**: 300 MHz to 300 GHz
**Wavelength**: 1 mm to 1 m
**Energy per photon**: ~10⁻³ eV

### Microwave Oven

Microwaves at ~2.45 GHz are absorbed by water molecules. The molecules' natural resonance frequency matches the microwave frequency → molecules vibrate rapidly → heat.

```
MICROWAVE ABSORPTION:
  
  H₂O molecule: naturally rotates/vibrates at microwave frequencies
  
  Microwave hits molecule → molecule absorbs energy → spins faster
  → kinetic energy increases → temperature rises
  
  Penetrates food ~1-5 cm → heats throughout (not just surface)
```

### Radar

RADAR = Radio Detection And Ranging.

```
RADAR PRINCIPLE:
  
  Transmit microwave pulse → bounces off target → returns
  
  Distance = (c × travel time) / 2
  
  Doppler shift of frequency → target speed
  
  Applications: weather radar, air traffic control, speed guns
```

### 5G and Satellite Communication

Millimeter waves (30–300 GHz) are used for 5G networks (high bandwidth, short range) and satellite communications.

## 60.4 Infrared Radiation

**Frequency**: 300 GHz to 400 THz
**Wavelength**: 700 nm to 1 mm
**Energy per photon**: ~0.001 to 1.7 eV

### Thermal Radiation

Every warm object emits infrared radiation. The hotter the object, the more it emits and the shorter the peak wavelength.

```
WIEN'S DISPLACEMENT LAW:
  λ_max × T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K
  
  Human body (37°C = 310 K):
    λ_max = 2.898×10⁻³ / 310 ≈ 9.4 μm  (mid-infrared)
    
  Sun surface (5800 K):
    λ_max = 2.898×10⁻³ / 5800 ≈ 500 nm  (visible green!)
    
  This is why infrared cameras can "see" people in the dark.
```

### Applications

- **Thermal imaging**: night vision, medical diagnosis, building insulation surveys
- **Remote controls**: TV remotes use near-infrared LEDs (~940 nm)
- **Fiber optic communication**: near-infrared (1300-1550 nm) travels well through glass
- **Heating elements**: infrared heaters, grills
- **Greenhouse effect**: Earth absorbs sunlight, re-emits IR; CO₂ absorbs IR → traps heat

## 60.5 Visible Light

**Frequency**: 400–790 THz
**Wavelength**: 380–700 nm
**Energy per photon**: 1.7–3.1 eV

This is the narrow band our eyes evolved to detect (because the Sun peaks here).

```
COLORS AND WAVELENGTHS:

  Color    Wavelength (nm)   Frequency (THz)
  -------  ---------------   ---------------
  Red      700–620           430–480
  Orange   620–590           480–510
  Yellow   590–570           510–530
  Green    570–495           530–610
  Blue     495–450           610–670
  Indigo   450–425           670–700
  Violet   425–380           700–790
```

### White Light and Dispersion

White light is a mixture of all visible wavelengths. When it passes through a prism (or raindrops), it separates into colors because different wavelengths refract at slightly different angles.

```
PRISM DISPERSION:
  
  White light →  prism →  rainbow (red to violet)
  
  Glass has different refractive index for different λ:
    n_violet > n_red (violet bends more)
    
  This is why rainbows are curved and have order R-O-Y-G-B-I-V
```

## 60.6 Ultraviolet Radiation

**Frequency**: 800 THz to 30 PHz
**Wavelength**: 10–380 nm
**Energy per photon**: 3.1–100 eV

### UV and Health

UV is divided into:
- **UV-A** (320–380 nm): causes tanning, some DNA damage
- **UV-B** (290–320 nm): sunburn, vitamin D synthesis, significant DNA damage
- **UV-C** (100–290 nm): extremely dangerous; mostly blocked by ozone layer

```
OZONE LAYER:
  
  O₃ (ozone) molecules absorb UV-B and UV-C:
  
  UV + O₃ → O₂ + O (breaking the ozone)
  
  Without the ozone layer, UV radiation reaching Earth would
  destroy DNA in all surface life.
  
  The "ozone hole" (thinning due to CFCs) increases UV-B at surface.
```

### Applications

- **Sterilization**: UV-C kills bacteria and viruses (UV germicidal lamps)
- **Fluorescence**: "black lights" cause fluorescent materials to glow
- **Forensics**: fingerprints, counterfeit detection
- **Photolithography**: UV light patterns microchips (shorter λ → finer features)

## 60.7 X-rays

**Frequency**: 30 PHz to 30 EHz
**Wavelength**: 0.01–10 nm
**Energy per photon**: 100 eV to 100 keV

### Production

X-rays are produced when fast electrons decelerate rapidly (bremsstrahlung radiation) or when electrons drop to inner atomic orbitals.

```
X-RAY TUBE:
  
  Electron gun → accelerates electrons to high speed (30-150 kV)
  → hits tungsten target
  → sudden deceleration → X-ray emitted
  
  Also: electrons knock inner-shell electrons out of tungsten atoms
  → outer electrons fill the gap → emit characteristic X-rays (specific frequencies)
```

### Medical X-rays

X-rays penetrate soft tissue but are absorbed by dense material (bone, metal):

```
X-RAY IMAGE:

  X-ray beam → patient → detector (film or digital)
  
  Bone (calcium): high Z, high density → absorbs more → appears WHITE on film
  Soft tissue: low absorption → appears grey
  Air (lungs): very low absorption → appears BLACK
```

### X-ray Diffraction

X-ray wavelengths (~0.1 nm) are comparable to atomic spacing in crystals. They diffract off crystal planes → diffraction pattern → reveals crystal structure.

This was used by Rosalind Franklin to determine the structure of DNA (1952).

## 60.8 Gamma Rays

**Frequency**: above 30 EHz
**Wavelength**: below 0.01 nm
**Energy per photon**: above 100 keV

### Source

Gamma rays are produced by nuclear transitions (decay of radioactive nuclei) and particle-antiparticle annihilation.

```
GAMMA DECAY:
  
  Excited nucleus* → ground state nucleus + γ photon
  
  Example:  Co-60* → Co-60 + γ (1.17 MeV) + γ (1.33 MeV)
```

### Biological Effects

Gamma rays are extremely penetrating and ionizing. They can damage DNA → radiation sickness, cancer. But also:

- **Cancer treatment (radiotherapy)**: focused gamma beams kill tumor cells
- **Gamma knife surgery**: multiple beams cross at a single focus in the brain
- **Food irradiation**: kills bacteria in food (safe, leaves no radioactivity)
- **Sterilization**: medical equipment sterilization

### Penetrating Power

```
PENETRATION OF RADIATION:

  Paper|Aluminum|Lead
  -----|--------|----
  α: stops here
      β: stops here
               γ: mostly stopped here, but some passes through
               
  Gamma rays need thick lead or dense concrete to be stopped.
```

## 60.9 Properties Common to All EM Waves

All electromagnetic waves, regardless of frequency:

```
1. TRAVEL AT c in vacuum:
   c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (2.998...)
   
2. ARE TRANSVERSE WAVES:
   E and B perpendicular to each other and to direction of travel
   
3. CARRY ENERGY:
   Energy flux (intensity) = Power/Area
   I = ε₀cE₀²/2
   
4. CARRY MOMENTUM:
   p = E/c (radiation pressure exists!)
   
5. DON'T NEED A MEDIUM:
   Travel through vacuum (unlike sound)
   
6. CAN BE REFLECTED, REFRACTED, DIFFRACTED, POLARIZED, AND INTERFERED
   
7. RELATIONSHIP: c = f × λ
```

### Reflection and Refraction

At an interface between two media:

```
REFLECTION: angle of incidence = angle of reflection
REFRACTION: Snell's Law: n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)

n = c/v = refractive index (how much slower light travels in medium)
```

## 60.10 The Wave-Particle Duality of Light

Light behaves as both a wave AND a particle (photon).

**As a wave**: diffraction, interference, polarization
**As a particle (photon)**: photoelectric effect, Compton scattering

```
PHOTON ENERGY:
  
  E = hf = hc/λ
  
  h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s  (Planck's constant)
  
  Higher frequency → more energetic photons
  
  Visible light photon: ~2 eV
  X-ray photon: ~10 keV
  Gamma ray photon: ~1 MeV
```

This wave-particle duality is resolved by quantum mechanics, but the dual nature is experimentally undeniable.

## Summary

- All EM waves travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s; differ only in frequency/wavelength
- **c = fλ** — fundamental relationship
- **Radio**: ~kHz to GHz; broadcasting, WiFi, radar
- **Microwave**: GHz; cooking (water resonance), mobile communications
- **Infrared**: THz; thermal radiation, remote controls, fiber optics
- **Visible**: 400-700 nm; what eyes detect; colors from red (long λ) to violet (short λ)
- **Ultraviolet**: ionizing, DNA damage, ozone absorption; sterilization, fluorescence
- **X-rays**: penetrates tissue, absorbed by bone; medical imaging, crystallography
- **Gamma rays**: highest energy; nuclear decay, cancer treatment, sterilization
- All EM waves: transverse, can travel in vacuum, carry energy and momentum
- **Photon energy**: E = hf; higher frequency = more energetic

## Key Equations

```
Wave equation:
  c = f × λ
  c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

Photon energy:
  E = hf = hc/λ
  h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s
  1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Wien's Displacement Law (peak wavelength):
  λ_max × T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K

Stefan-Boltzmann Law (total power radiated):
  P = σ × A × T⁴
  σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴)

Refractive index:
  n = c/v
  Snell's Law: n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)

Intensity (radiation pressure):
  I = P/A  (W/m²)
  Radiation pressure = I/c
```
