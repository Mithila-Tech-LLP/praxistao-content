## 1. Why Nickel for Hostile Environments?

**Nickel's key properties:**
- FCC structure (stable from cryogenic to melting point at 1,455°C — no allotropic transformation → no brittleness risk like BCC iron)
- High melting point → still has useful strength at 1,000°C
- Excellent resistance to reducing acids (hydrochloric, sulfuric) and alkalis
- Strong oxide film at high temperature (Cr addition → Cr₂O₃)
- Can accept large amounts of alloying elements (Al, Ti, Co, Cr, Mo, W) in solid solution + precipitates → superalloys

**Nickel's role in corrosion resistance:**
- Pure Ni: resists reducing acids + alkalis + many salts
- Ni + Cr: adds resistance to oxidizing environments (Cr₂O₃ passivation)
- Ni + Mo: resists HCl and other reducing acids
- Ni + Cu: seawater and non-oxidizing acid resistance

## 2. Nickel Alloy Classification

```
Nickel alloys
├── Commercially pure Ni
├── Ni-Cu (Monel): seawater, reducing acids
├── Ni-Cr (Inconel 600 series): high T oxidation
├── Ni-Cr-Mo (Inconel 625, Hastelloy C276): extreme corrosion
├── Ni-Mo (Hastelloy B): HCl resistance
├── Ni-Cr-Fe (Incoloy 800): intermediate temperature
└── Superalloys (Inconel 718, CMSX series): gas turbines (see Ch 42-49)
```

## 3. Commercially Pure Nickel

**Nickel 200 (N02200: 99.6% Ni):**
- σ_y = 100–400 MPa depending on condition
- Excellent resistance to caustic (NaOH, KOH) at all concentrations and temperatures
- Food processing equipment (caustic evaporators, neutralizers)
- Chemical plant (high pH environments)
- Electronics (plating anode, battery electrodes)

**Nickel 201 (N02201: 99.6% Ni, max 0.02% C):**
- Lower carbon than Ni 200 → prevents graphite precipitation → better for welded constructions
- Caustic service above 315°C

## 4. Nickel-Copper Alloys — Monel

**Monel 400 (N04400: 67Ni-31Cu-1.4Fe-1.2Mn):**
- σ_y = 170–620 MPa (annealed to cold worked)
- Excellent corrosion resistance in:
  - Seawater (even at high velocity — no SCC like stainless)
  - Hydrofluoric acid (unique among metals — Ti and stainless attack)
  - Neutral and reducing acids
- Used for: heat exchanger tubes, pump shafts, valve seats in chemical plants, marine hardware

**Monel K-500 (N05500: Ni-Cu + 3%Al, 0.6%Ti):**
- Precipitation hardened (Ni₃(Al,Ti) precipitates)
- σ_y = 700 MPa, STA condition
- Combines Monel 400 corrosion resistance + high strength
- Used for: pump impellers and shafts, propeller shafts in seawater, marine hardware under load

## 5. Nickel-Chromium Alloys — Inconel Family

**Inconel 600 (N06600: 76Ni-15.5Cr-8Fe):**
- Classic Ni-Cr alloy for high-temperature oxidation resistance
- Max temperature: 1,150°C in oxidizing atmosphere
- Excellent resistance to: seawater, oxidizing + reducing acids at moderate T, chloride SCC
- Used for: furnace components, heat treating baskets, heat exchangers for high-T chemical reactions

**Inconel 625 (N06625: 61Ni-21.5Cr-9Mo-3.6Nb + Al, Ti, Fe):**
- No heat treatment — high solid solution strength (Cr + Mo + Nb in FCC Ni)
- σ_y = 414 MPa (annealed), excellent fatigue and creep to 870°C
- Outstanding corrosion resistance even in seawater (Mo + Nb)
- Used for: offshore umbilical tubing, chemical plant piping, aircraft bellows, welding cladding of pipelines

**Inconel 718 (N07718: 52Ni-19Cr-5Nb-3Mo-1Ti-0.6Al + Fe):**
- The most widely used superalloy
- Precipitation hardened by γ″ (Ni₃Nb, D0₂₂) and γ′ (Ni₃(Al,Ti), L1₂)
- γ″ has more volume fraction but dissolves above 650°C
- T = 650°C: σ_y = 1,034 MPa (room T aging), σ_UTS = 1,241 MPa
- Used for: jet engine disks and cases up to 650°C (very weldable for a superalloy — doesn't crack)

**Inconel 690 (N06690: 58Ni-29Cr-9Fe):**
- Very high Cr content → excellent resistance to Cr₂O₃-forming environment
- Low C, high Cr → resistant to sensitization
- Nuclear steam generators (pressurized water reactors) — replaced 600 after 600 SCC incidents

## 6. Nickel-Molybdenum Alloys — Hastelloy Family

**Hastelloy B-3 (N10675: 65Ni-28.5Mo-1.5Cr-1.5Fe):**
- Best resistance to HCl (hydrochloric acid) at all concentrations and temperatures
- Resistance to H₂SO₄, H₃PO₄
- Used for: HCl chemical plant reactors, condensers
- WARNING: Hastelloy B is susceptible to SCC in oxidizing acid mixtures (Fe³⁺ or Cu²⁺ contaminated HCl) — specify B-3 which has better stability

**Hastelloy C-276 (N10276: 57Ni-15.5Mo-15.5Cr-5.5Fe-3.8W):**
- Excellent resistance to oxidizing AND reducing environments (Cr + Mo combined)
- Resistant to: seawater, H₂SO₄, HCl, HNO₃, chlorine dioxide, many organic acids
- "The universal corrosion alloy" — used when you don't know exactly what the environment is
- Standard material for chemical plant piping in multi-service environments

**Hastelloy X (N06002: 47Ni-21Cr-18Fe-9Mo-0.6W):**
- Optimized for HIGH-TEMPERATURE oxidation resistance (to 1,200°C)
- Excellent strength at 870–980°C in gas turbine combustors
- Used for: jet engine combustion liners, afterburner components, industrial furnace equipment
- Note: this is the transition between "corrosion alloys" and "superalloys"

## 7. Nickel Superalloys (Overview)

Superalloys are the highest-performance class — covered in detail in Chapters 42–60. Key distinction:

| Property | Hastelloy/Inconel type | Superalloys (IN100, CMSX-4) |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| Service temperature | 600–1,000°C | 900–1,100°C |
| Strengthening | Solid solution + some precipitation | γ/γ′ precipitation (dominant) |
| Heat treatment | Simple anneal | Complex solution + multi-step age |
| Manufacturing | Standard wrought/cast | DS or SX casting only for HPT |
| Cost | $50–200/kg | $500–3,000/kg |

## 8. Cobalt-Based Alloys — Stellite and Haynes

Cobalt alloys are used where:
- BOTH high temperature AND abrasion/wear resistance is needed
- Sliding wear in high-temperature environments
- Corrosion resistance combined with wear

**Why cobalt?**
- FCC structure to ~422°C, then HCP → low stacking fault energy → wide stacking faults → difficult cross-slip → work hardening at high T → wear resistance
- High melting point (1,495°C)
- Cr gives oxidation/corrosion resistance
- C + W/Mo → carbide network for wear

**Stellite 6 (CoCrW alloy, ~Co-28Cr-4.5W-1.2C):**
- High carbon → Cr₂₃C₆ and WC/W₂C carbides → EXCEPTIONAL wear and erosion resistance
- Maintains hardness at 650°C
- Used for: valve seats and faces, gas turbine blade erosion shield overlays, drill bits, cutting tools for difficult materials
- Typically applied as welded-on overlay or thermal spray coating

**Stellite 21 / Haynes 25 (L-605) (Co-20Cr-15W-10Ni-3Fe-0.1C):**
- Lower C → fewer carbides → better ductility and weldability
- High W and Cr → hot strength
- Used for: surgical implants (Stellite 21 is biocompatible), heart valve hardware, dental prosthetics

**Haynes 230 (Co-22Cr-14W-2Mo-Mn-Si-low C):**
- Excellent cyclic oxidation resistance at 1,150°C
- Used for: industrial gas turbine transition ducts, combustors, high-temperature furnace rollers

**Haynes 188 (Co-22Cr-22Ni-14W):**
- Balanced Co-Ni alloy
- Excellent high-temperature strength + oxidation to 1,100°C
- Gas turbine combustion cans, afterburner hardware

### Cobalt vs. Nickel for High Temperature:
| Property | Cobalt alloys | Nickel superalloys |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| Max T | ~1,000°C | ~1,100°C+ |
| Wear resistance | Excellent (carbides) | Poor |
| Hot hardness | Good | Moderate |
| Strength at T | Lower than γ/γ′ Ni | Much higher |
| Medical use | Yes (Stellite, biocompatible) | Limited (Ni allergy) |

Cobalt alloys are chosen for WEAR + TEMPERATURE combination (valve seats, erosion), not for maximum creep strength (that's nickel superalloys).

## Exercises

1. A chemical plant processes HCl gas mixed with trace H₂SO₄ and occasionally has traces of oxidizing contamination (Fe³⁺). The process temperature is 150°C. Compare: (a) Hastelloy B-3, (b) Hastelloy C-276, (c) Monel 400, (d) Inconel 625. Which alloy(s) would survive, and which would fail? Explain the corrosion mechanism for each failure.

2. Inconel 718 is used for jet engine compressor disks and fasteners. (a) Explain why IN718 is "weldable" while most superalloys (like CMSX-4) are not (hint: no Al+Ti > 3%, so no strain-age cracking). (b) IN718 loses strength above 650°C because γ″ dissolves. Sketch the γ″ solvus and explain why this limits IN718 to 650°C while PWA 1484 (SX, γ′-hardened) operates to 1,100°C. (c) The disk cost is $50,000 and must be replaced after 20,000 cycles. A new alloy (ATI 718Plus) extends life to 30,000 cycles. Calculate the per-flight cost saving.

3. Stellite 6 hard-facing is applied to a steam valve seat by plasma transferred arc (PTA) welding. (a) What microstructure would you expect in the Stellite overlay? (identify the matrix and carbide phases) (b) After 5 years of service, the valve leaks due to wear. On cross-section, you see smooth wear on the stellite and rough pitting on the stainless steel counterface. Explain this damage mechanism. (c) Calculate the expected wear life if the Stellite overlay is increased from 3mm to 5mm, assuming linear wear rate.

4. Hastelloy C-276 piping is being specified for a desulfurization unit handling wet H₂S + HCl + HNO₃ mixtures at 200°C. The mechanical specification requires σ_y > 250 MPa. (a) Does solution-annealed C-276 meet the strength requirement? (b) Can C-276 be precipitation-hardened to increase strength? (c) If higher strength is needed, what alloy modification could help while maintaining corrosion resistance? (d) What are the welding specifications (filler metal, heat input, PWHT) for C-276 to maintain its corrosion resistance in the HAZ?

5. A biomedical knee implant must withstand 10 million cycles in synovial fluid at 37°C, withstand 3,000 N peak load, have high corrosion resistance to body fluids, and minimize Ni ion release (some patients have Ni allergy). Compare: (a) Ti-6Al-4V ELI, (b) Co-Cr-Mo alloy (ASTM F75), (c) 316L stainless steel. For each: comment on σ_y, wear resistance, biocompatibility, and potential for Ni release. Which would you choose for the femoral component (highest wear), and which for the tibial tray (lower wear, must bond to bone cement)?
