# Chapter 26: Copper Alloys — Conductors, Springs, and Marine Metal

> **"Copper was humanity's first metal, worked for over 10,000 years. Long before iron or aluminum, copper and its alloys — bronze and brass — defined the Bronze Age and, by extension, the course of civilization. Today, copper remains essential not for its strength, but for its unmatched electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, and corrosion resistance in water and seawater. A world without copper would be a world without electricity."**

## 1. Copper's Fundamental Properties

| Property | Copper | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Density | 8.96 g/cm³ | Heavy |
| Melting point | 1,085°C | Moderate |
| Electrical conductivity | 100% IACS | Best of any practical metal |
| Thermal conductivity | 401 W/m·K | 2nd to silver |
| Yield strength (pure) | 70 MPa | Very soft — easily cold worked |
| FCC crystal structure | — | 12 slip systems → very ductile |
| Corrosion in water | Excellent | Patina (basic copper carbonate) |
| Color | Distinctive orange-red | Decorative use |

**Why copper for electrical wires:** Every other metal is either more expensive (Ag) or less conductive (Al = 61% IACS, Fe = 17% IACS). For power-critical applications (generator windings, integrated circuits), copper is essentially irreplaceable.

## 2. Pure Copper Grades

| Grade | Designation | Purity | Key Use |
|-------|------------|--------|---------|
| ETP copper | C11000 | 99.90% | Electrical wire, bus bars |
| OFHC copper | C10200 | 99.95%, oxygen-free | Vacuum tubes, waveguides, plating |
| DHP copper | C12200 | 99.85%, deoxidized | Plumbing tubes, roofing |
| Tellurium copper | C14500 | 99.4% + 0.5%Te | Machining (Te improves machinability) |

**ETP copper (C11000):** Most common; 99.90% Cu + ~0.04% O (as Cu₂O inclusions). Good electrical conductivity (97% IACS after annealing). Can be embrittled by H₂ at elevated T ("hydrogen disease" — H₂ reduces Cu₂O → steam → cracking). OFHC avoids this for hydrogen-atmosphere applications.

## 3. Brass — Copper-Zinc Alloys

Brass is Cu-Zn alloy. Properties vary dramatically with Zn content:

### Alpha Brass (< 36% Zn)

Single-phase FCC. Good ductility, formable, weldable.

**70/30 Brass (C26000, "Cartridge Brass"):**
- 70% Cu – 30% Zn
- σ_y = 103 MPa (annealed), 530 MPa (fully cold worked)
- Excellent deep drawing ability
- Used in: cartridge cases (hence the name), plumbing fittings, musical instruments, radiator cores, springs

**85/15 Brass (C23000, "Red Brass"):**
- Reddish color, good corrosion resistance in water
- Plumbing, architectural hardware

### Alpha + Beta Brass (36–45% Zn)

Two-phase (α + β). Less ductile than α brass. Better machinability.

**60/40 Brass (C28000, "Muntz Metal"):**
- Stronger (β phase), less ductile
- Architectural cladding, ship sheathing, condenser plates

### Leaded Brass (Alpha + Pb)

Small Pb additions (1–3%) dramatically improve machinability:
- Pb is insoluble in brass → forms discrete particles → chip-breaker in machining
- **C36000 (Free-Cutting Brass):** 61.5%Cu-35.5%Zn-3%Pb → best machinability of any copper alloy
- Used for: turned parts, screw machine products, fittings — millions of valves, connectors

**RESTRICTION:** EU RoHS directive restricts Pb content to < 4% in plumbing applications. Lead-free alternatives being developed (Bi-brass, Si-brass).

### Dezincification Risk

Alpha-beta brass (40%+ Zn) is susceptible to **dezincification** — selective corrosion of Zn → porous Cu plug remains:
- Occurs in hot, slightly acidic water
- Avoided by: using α-brass (< 36% Zn), or adding 0.04%As (inhibited brass)

### Season Cracking — Stress Corrosion Cracking in Brass

70/30 cartridge brass cases under residual tensile stress + ammonia → SCC (discovered in India, where cases cracked in horse stables):
- Mechanism: dissolved NH₃ → attacks grain boundaries under stress
- Prevented by: stress relief anneal at 260°C after forming

## 4. Bronze — Copper-Tin and Others

### Tin Bronze (Cu-Sn)

Classical bronze. High strength, excellent corrosion resistance, good bearing properties.

**Phosphor Bronze (C51000: Cu-5Sn-0.2P):**
- P added for deoxidation → small amounts remain → slight strengthening
- Excellent spring properties: high fatigue strength, good electrical conductivity
- Used for: springs, bellows, switch contacts, musical instruments (bells have specific Sn content for tone)

**Bearing Bronze (C93200: 83Cu-7Sn-7Pb-3Zn):**
- Pb provides dry lubrication (smears on bearing surface)
- High load-bearing capacity
- Self-lubricating bushings, bearings, worm gears

### Aluminum Bronze (Cu-Al, 5–12% Al)

Al forms Al₂O₃ film → excellent corrosion resistance; Al dissolves in Cu → solid solution strengthening:

**C63000 (Al-Ni Bronze: Cu-10Al-5Ni-3Fe):**
- σ_y = 415 MPa, σ_UTS = 760 MPa — strongest copper alloy (except beryllium copper)
- Excellent seawater corrosion resistance (no dezincification risk)
- Used for: marine propellers, seawater pump impellers, offshore deck fittings, aircraft landing gear bushings

### Silicon Bronze (Cu-Si, 1–4% Si)

Strong, highly corrosion-resistant, excellent welding characteristics:
- C65500: Cu-3Si, σ_y = 160 MPa (annealed), excellent chemical equipment
- Good for welded chemical storage tanks

## 5. Cupronickel — Marine Alloys

Cu-Ni alloys are completely miscible (Ni and Cu have similar radii, both FCC). Excellent corrosion resistance in seawater.

**90/10 Cupronickel (C70600: 90Cu-10Ni-1.5Fe-1Mn):**
- 90% Cu + 10% Ni + Fe, Mn for biofouling resistance
- σ_y = 105 MPa; excellent seawater corrosion
- Used in: naval vessel heat exchanger tubes, condenser tubes, seawater pipelines, coin metal (5-cent nickel in US: 75%Cu-25%Ni)

**70/30 Cupronickel (C71500):**
- Better corrosion resistance than 90/10, especially at elevated velocity (erosion)
- Used for: submarine sonar domes, desalination evaporators, high-performance marine heat exchangers

**Why cupronickel in seawater:** Unlike stainless steel (susceptible to crevice corrosion and pitting in stagnant seawater), cupronickel forms a strongly adherent Cu₂O + NiO film that resists attack even at high flow velocity (erosion-corrosion). The Cu toxicity also inhibits biofouling.

## 6. Beryllium Copper — The Strongest Copper Alloy

**The premium copper alloy.** 2% Be in Cu → precipitation hardening (Cu₃Be precipitates) → enormous strength:

**C17200 (Cu-2Be):**
- Solution treat at 800°C → quench → age at 315°C
- Peak aged: σ_y = 1,100 MPa, σ_UTS = 1,380 MPa, 6% elongation
- Hardness: 38–45 HRC
- Still excellent electrical conductivity: 22% IACS (much better than steel, comparable to some bronzes)
- K_Ic ≈ 50 MPa√m (adequate)

**Unique combination:**
- High strength (1,100 MPa) AND good conductivity (22% IACS)
- No other metal combines both at this level
- Non-sparking (critical for explosive environments — mining, chemical plants)

**Applications:**
- Miniature electronic springs and contacts (camera shutters, connector pins, springs in electronics)
- Non-sparking tools (screwdrivers, wrenches for refineries)
- Molds for plastic injection (rapid heat extraction → fast cycle time)
- High-performance springs where space is limited

**HAZARD:** Beryllium dust is TOXIC (causes berylliosis — chronic lung disease). Strict OSHA exposure limits (0.2 μg/m³). Machining and grinding of Be-Cu requires enclosed systems and respiratory protection.

## 8. Processing Copper Alloys

**Melting and casting:**
- EAF or induction furnace
- Covered melting (prevent O₂, H₂ pickup)
- DC casting or continuous casting

**Hot working:** 700–900°C; sluggish in β-brass above 600°C; lead segregation in leaded brass

**Cold working:** Excellent formability; Cu alloys work harden significantly
- Deep drawing: 70/30 brass → cartridge cases (50% reduction per pass with annealing)
- Spring rolling: phosphor bronze, beryllium copper → high elastic spring limit

**Heat treatment:**
- Annealing: 300–700°C depending on alloy
- Age hardening: beryllium copper (800°C ST, 315°C age)
- Stress relief: 260°C/1h for brass springs (prevent SCC)

**Joining:**
- Soldering: excellent (good wettability by Sn-Pb or Pb-free solders)
- Brazing: BAg (silver-base) or BCuP (Cu-phosphorus) filler
- Welding: good (gas, GTAW) for Cu and Cu-rich alloys; brass welding loses Zn (fume)

## Exercises

1. A marine heat exchanger must transfer heat from seawater (flowing at 2 m/s) to a process fluid. Compare: (a) 316L stainless steel tubes, (b) 90/10 cupronickel tubes, (c) titanium Grade 2 tubes. For each: comment on corrosion resistance, biofouling, thermal conductivity (k_316L = 16, k_CuNi = 29, k_Ti = 22 W/m·K), and relative cost. Which would you choose for a 30-year service life?

2. A spring connector in a consumer electronics device requires: spring constant 50 N/mm, minimum deflection 5 mm, length < 15 mm, must pass 10⁷ cycles without failure, electrical resistance < 50 mΩ. Compare: (a) music wire (0.8% C spring steel), (b) phosphor bronze C51000, (c) beryllium copper C17200. For each: estimate achievable σ_y, fatigue limit, electrical resistivity (inverse conductivity), and suitability. Which meets all requirements?

3. Dezincification failure has occurred in 40/60 brass (40%Zn) hot water fittings. (a) Describe the dezincification mechanism (selective Zn dissolution). (b) How would you verify dezincification: what does the fitting cross-section look like? (c) Three corrective actions: (i) change to inhibited brass (what inhibitor?), (ii) change alloy to α-brass (what maximum Zn content?), (iii) change alloy entirely (what would you use?). For each, explain the metallurgical basis.

4. Beryllium copper C17200 is being machined in a job shop. The operator is grinding the hardened part without respiratory protection. (a) What health risk is present? (b) What OSHA-mandated exposure limit must not be exceeded? (c) List three engineering controls that must be implemented. (d) The customer wants to replace Be-Cu with a non-toxic alternative. What is the highest strength non-Be copper alloy available, and how does its strength compare?

5. 70/30 cartridge brass cases are manufactured by deep drawing (multiple passes, annealing between passes). After final sizing, cases have residual tensile stress of ~200 MPa on outer surface. (a) What environment will cause stress corrosion cracking of these cases? (b) What is the time-to-failure relationship (hint: SCC is time-dependent — depends on KIscc)? (c) The military specification requires stress relief at 260°C for 1 hour. After stress relief, residual stress drops to ~30 MPa. Explain why this anneal is subcritical (doesn't change strength) but effectively prevents SCC. (d) Calculate the diffusion distance of Zn at 260°C for 1 hour (D_Zn ≈ 10⁻¹⁸ m²/s at 260°C). Does any significant compositional change occur?
